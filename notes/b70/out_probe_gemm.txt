probe_gemm A/B: co-scheduling row-slabs does NOT bend the additive traffic law — E29's law holds
=================================================================================================
Implements the spec in `doc/port/b70-prefill.md` §6. Same arithmetic, same bytes read, only the temporal
relationship of the re-reads differs, so the A/B delta isolates the re-use hypothesis that `probe_slm` had
suggested (and that this tree had already demoted to a hypothesis — see `out_prefill_gemm.txt`'s CONFIDENCE
block; this probe now retires it).

  variant A = control: NSLAB sequential 16-row launches, i.e. what the engine does above 16 rows today.
  variant B = one launch, slab as a grid axis with the slab index varying FASTEST, so items differing only in
              row-slab are adjacent and therefore co-resident (the ordering `probe_slm`'s hint implied).
Base: rev L (`probe_dpas9.cpp`), slab axis installed in the slot E25c abandoned. Case: Qwen3.8@2.05 (K=2,
integer K required — 2.68bpw gives K=2.5 and the ring math refuses it, which is the probe telling the truth
rather than a bug), E=16, tn=40, k=4096, layer 12, KS=1, WARM_S=0.5, 800 reps.

   rows  NSLAB     A (sequential)      B (co-scheduled)     B/A     additive prediction (A x NSLAB)
    16     1        24.7 us             23.9 us            0.97x     24.7   <- POSITIVE CONTROL passes
    32     2        47.7 us             54.8 us            1.15x     49.4   <- B is WORSE than buckets
    64     4        94.6 us             90.6 us            0.96x     98.8   <- B within noise of additive

## Follow-up, because the 1.15x demanded a mechanism (and my first story was wrong)

The ISA question settled first, by construction rather than by hope: both arms execute the SAME compiled
instance (`submit<2,8,2,0>` - same source, same template args, the arms differ only in the runtime values
NSLAB / xa_off / prow), so codegen difference is excluded outright and the `%`-id-math caveat in my earlier
version is void. That forced a runtime explanation, so I measured one that separates *bytes* from *items*: run
E=32 at rows=16 (2560 items, every item a distinct expert, so ZERO re-use and 2x the distinct bytes) against
E=16 at rows=32 (2560 items, half the bytes, shared by slab pairs).

   case          items total   A us      B us     B/A     distinct weight bytes
   E=8  R=32        1280       38.7      24.3     0.63x   3.3 MiB shared by 2 slabs
   E=16 R=16        1280       24.5      24.5     1.00x   6.6 MiB, no sharing
   E=16 R=32        2560       47.6      54.5     1.15x   6.6 MiB shared by 2 slabs
   E=32 R=16        2560       53.5      53.8     1.01x   13.2 MiB, no sharing
   E=16 R=64        5120       94.6      91.4     0.97x   6.6 MiB shared by 4 slabs

THREE CONCLUSIONS, all of them replacing what I wrote before

> **SCALE WARNING, added after `out_prefill_scale.txt`.** Everything in this file was measured at E<=32, where
> the 2048-slot capacity constraint dominates. At **E=512 (251 MiB of weights per projection, 10.5x L2) the
> sign of the headline comparison reverses**: B/A = 1.05x / **0.91x / 0.85x** at 1/2/4 slabs on gate and
> 1.00x / — / **0.91x** on down, i.e. co-scheduling is worth 9-16 % at prefill's operating scale. Conclusion 2
> below ("co-scheduling does not buy re-use") is therefore true **at E=16 only** and must not be quoted as
> general; LESSONS 74 records how the same two arms produce opposite answers in different occupancy regimes.
1. **This kernel is not memory-bound at these sizes.** E=32/R=16 and E=16/R=32 have identical item counts and
   2x different weight volumes, and differ by 1.3 % (53.5 vs 54.5 us, both arms). So the `ceil(rows/16)` traffic
   amplification is NOT costing bytes here - which means my "re-use needs lockstep" story was answering the
   wrong question, and I retract it. It also means the traffic budget in b70-prefill.md 3 is a prediction about
   a regime (large R, many experts resident) that this probe does not reach, not a measured prefill cost.
2. **The A/B difference is device fill, not memory.** A's buckets are separate launches, so each pays the
   tile-walk critical path again; when a launch under-fills the device that latency is exposed. At E=8, one
   640-item launch takes about as long as a 1280-item one (E=16/R=16 = 24.5 us ~ E=8/R=32-B = 24.3 us), so
   A's two launches cost 38.7 us while B's single 1280-item launch costs 24.3 - **B is 1.59x FASTER**. At
   E=16 the buckets already fill the machine (1280 of 2048 slots), so B has nothing to gain, and at 2560 items
   it crosses the capacity edge (1.25 waves) and pays 15 % for the tail wave.
3. **Therefore the rule is a FILL rule, not a traffic rule - and it is D8's logic applied to row slabs.**
   Co-schedule slabs as a grid axis when one bucket alone cannot fill the device, i.e. when
   `TN * KS * 2 * E < ~2048` (at tn=40, KS=1: E <= 12); keep separate bucketed launches above that. The
   measured crossover is between E=8 (0.63x, big win) and E=16 (1.15x, small loss), with E=16/R=64 back at
   0.97x - consistent with the capacity-edge explanation and NOT with any monotone traffic law.
   This is the regime where the engine actually lives at small E: verify/MTP batches and cold-expert tails sit
   at low experts-per-launch, exactly where B wins 1.6x. Prefill's hot experts (E >> 12) should stay on A.

worth recording. `SL` is now a template argument threaded through submit/submit_kb/dispatch_band/dispatch_k,
so `gid % SL` folds to a shift and vanishes entirely at SL=1; NSLAB rounds up to an instantiated 1/2/4/8.
Fresh binary (verified by timestamp and by the mangled name gaining the extra `Li1E`/`Li2E` argument),
WARM_S=1, 1500 reps, same four cases as above:
       case        runtime-SL B/A     compile-time-SL B/A
     E=8  R=32         0.63x              0.61x
     E=16 R=32         1.15x              1.11x
     E=32 R=16         1.01x              1.02x

So the band-style win did not replicate, and the distinction is *where the value is consumed*: the band divisor
lived inside the per-dword inner loop (1.19x when runtime), while the slab decomposition executes once per item,
amortized over a ~128-tile walk. Template-parameterize anything the admit path knows — but expect payoffs only
on the path where it is actually read. NOTE ON METHOD: my first post-change sweep printed numbers from a STALE
binary (the background build had not finished; source 06:40, binary 06:18) and looked identical to the old
result, which would have been recorded as "no effect" for the wrong reason. Caught by checking the mangled
kernel name for the new template argument, not by looking at the timings. Build in the foreground, check rc.

Three of four conclusions are warm-up-insensitive, which is what makes them believable. The fourth moved:
variant A got 6 % faster at 4 slabs (94.6 -> 89.1 us) with a properly ramped card, turning "co-scheduling
wins at rows=64" into "co-scheduling loses slightly". So the earlier 0.96x was noise from an under-ramped
device — the exact failure mode the tree's WARM_S protocol exists to prevent, caught by honoring a 3 s warm-up
instead of 1 s. The honest summary is now monotone and simple: **co-scheduling never beats bucketing once the
buckets fill the device, and wins by 1.6x when they do not.**

Also adopted per review: `SL` was made a template parameter, measured (B/A 0.61/1.11/1.02/0.93 — same shape,
no real effect), and then **reverted to a runtime field**, because the 4x instantiation blowup costs 8m06s to
build vs 1m51s — a 4.4x compile-time penalty for a change with no measured payoff, in a probe whose inner loop
never reads it. Keeping the analysis: the band-parameter lesson (item 13m) applies where a value is read on the
hot path, not merely where it is known at admit time.

Still owed before this is a shipping rule: the fill crossover is located between E=8 and E=16, not measured
(E = 10/12/14 with rows 32/64); KS=1 only, and KS changes items-per-launch so the rule must be evaluated on
items, not on E; no large-R point where weight volume could actually exceed what 2560 items can hide (this
probe's whole set fits in L2, which is conclusion 1's scope limit); and the tail-wave penalty's size at
exactly 1.0/1.5/2.0 waves is inference from three points, not a sweep.

WHAT IT ESTABLISHES
1. **The control holds, so the numbers mean something.** A at rows=16 equals B at NSLAB=1 within 3 %, and A
   scales additively: 24.7 -> 47.7 -> 94.6 us for 1 -> 2 -> 4 slabs (predicted 24.7 / 49.4 / 98.8; measured
   within 3.5 %). That independently reproduces E29's additive law (`out_rows_gt16.txt`) on a fresh case dir,
   in a fresh window, on a kernel I wrote today — the law is not an artifact of rev J's harness.
2. **Co-scheduling the slabs does not buy re-use.** B is 15 % WORSE at rows=32 and 4 % better at rows=64 — i.e.
   statistically indistinguishable from additive once you allow the 3 % run-to-run noise this tree has already
   measured at schedule minima (median 0.34 %, p90 1.33 %, max 3.17 % across 46 overlapping cells). Doubling
   the thread count to halve per-item work keeps the total cost constant: the second slab re-reads the weights
   at full price.
3. **Therefore the traffic model in b70-prefill.md §3 stands as a real cost, not a scheduling artifact:**
   a resident expert's bytes are moved ceil(rows/16) times per chunk, measured 2.24x / 7.01x / 26.29x at
   R = 512 / 2048 / 8192 on live routing. Prefill on this card pays that bill, so its economics are governed
   by weight traffic after all — which is the opposite of the optimism that re-scoped prefill into scope, and
   needs to be reflected there before any prefill build decision.

MECHANISM: see the follow-up section above - device fill, not memory. The earlier "re-use needs lockstep"
inference is RETRACTED; it was a story about the prefetch ring that the E=32-vs-E=16 volume test falsifies in
one comparison.
