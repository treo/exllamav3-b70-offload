E25b: one combine launch for the whole fused pair - REFUTED
===========================================================
Question. After E25a the layer runs gemm(gate+up fused) -> combine(gate) -> combine(up) -> gemm(down) ->
combine(down). DPAS_FLOOR prices launch+setup+combine at ~7.8 us per projection at rows=1, i.e. more than half
of the rows=1 layer time is fixed cost. E25a showed fusion buys parallelism for the gemm. E25b asked whether it
buys anything for the combine too: merge the pair's two combines into one grid.

Why it looked promising. The two projections are independent, so unlike E29's row bucketing (which had to double
the accumulator set and died on the 128-GRF register file) a work-item still touches exactly one projection. The
extra state is one partial pointer and one output pointer selected by index: register bill flat, one dispatch
saved.

Result (fused gemm unchanged, gate+up on /tmp/gemv_R64 + /tmp/gemv_up_R64, KS=1, min of 5 interleaved rounds,
anchor rev I gate rows=1 = 19.4 us, healthy; both arms bit-exact against each projection's own y_ref, read from
the directory the run wrote):

  rows=1    two combines 21.50 us -> one combine 21.60 us    +0.10 us  (x0.995)
  rows=8    two combines 26.80 us -> one combine 28.70 us    +1.90 us  (x0.934)
  rows=16   two combines 34.60 us -> one combine 38.10 us    +3.50 us  (x0.908)

AMENDED THE SAME SESSION — the conclusion below is RETRACTED. Standalone (probe_combine.cpp, no gemm in the
timed region), the merged combine is ~2x FASTER, not slower: rows=1 2.87 vs split 5.89 us, rows=8 2.86 vs 5.92,
rows=16 3.76 vs 5.81. Split therefore costs ~2.9 us per launch - the protocol cost - and merging removes one of
them, exactly as hypothesized. Both measurements are clean, so the in-situ loss above is an INTERACTION with the
preceding fused gemm (L2 state of freshly written partials, or queue behaviour), not the combine kernel's cost.
"Launch-count reduction per se is not a lever" does not follow from any data here and is withdrawn; the lever is
real in isolation and its in-situ cancellation is an open question. ISA census (asm_e25b/, 4 dumps, 565-994
lines, mov 93-193, mul 27-53, no spill/scratch indications) does not identify which dump is which schedule, so
it adjudicates nothing.

FOLLOW-UP EXPERIMENT, same session — the dirty-L2 hypothesis is REFUTED too. probe_combine.cpp gained a
producer pass that rewrites the whole partial region immediately before the combines, so they are read from L2
dirty exactly as after a gemm epilogue (standalone, 200-rep bursts, min of 4 interleaved rounds):

  rows=1    clean: split 6.35  pair 3.14 (-3.21)   |  L2-dirty: split 6.71  pair 3.17 (-3.54)
  rows=8    clean: split 6.33  pair 3.15 (-3.18)   |  L2-dirty: split 6.67  pair 3.20 (-3.47)
  rows=16   clean: split 6.36  pair 3.74 (-2.62)   |  L2-dirty: split 7.01  pair 3.52 (-3.50)

Dirtiness costs the split form a little (~0.4-0.7 us) and the merged form nothing, so it widens the merged
form's advantage instead of cancelling it. Caveat on absolute values: the anchor read 26.2 us in this command
because it ran AFTER the sweep rather than interleaved with it — depressed, not healthy — so only the
within-row comparisons above are usable.

READING THE ISA, WHICH THE OPCODE CENSUS COULD NOT SAY. The mangled .kernel names identify the four dumps as
two split and two pair variants (the backend emitted a second specialization per schedule). All four do the same
work per item: decompose the flat index with `mul ... 0xa00` (= 640 dwords x 4 B, the row stride) plus `mulh`
for the division-by-constant, then 4 `lsc_load.ugm` (2 k-slice partials + Q + SX8) and 1 `lsc_store`, with the
second k-slice reached as an IMMEDIATE offset `flat[V0308+0x64000]` (0x64000 = 409,600 B = one KS-slice region)
in both split dumps AND in one pair dump. So the pointer select `PART = a ? PA : PB` does not defeat immediate
folding — my earlier guess, wrong — and the two schedules are ISA near-clones. Whatever inverts the sign in
situ is not in the combine's instructions.

WHERE THAT LEAVES E25b. The merged combine wins in every condition this probe can produce standalone (clean,
dirty, rows 1/8/16). It loses in situ (+0.10 / +1.90 / +3.50 us). The remaining difference between the two
setups is not the machine state left by the gemm, so the candidate is the measurement protocol: the in-situ
probe times bursts of 3 whole layer-iterations (6-7 launches deep per burst, with the ESIMD gemm's occupancy
ramp in the stream), while the standalone probe bursts 200 iterations of nothing but combines. The tie-breaker
is therefore a protocol test, not a hardware one: re-measure the in-situ arms at burst length 1 (sync per
iteration) as well as 3, which needs the merged arm reinstated in probe_dpas9 temporarily. Until that runs, the
kept schedule stays split because that is the only form measured in situ, but the honest expectation from the
standalone data is that split is the WRONG choice and the in-situ result is a scheduling artifact.

IN-SITU ISA, READ (asm_insite/) — the mechanism, not a guess. Dumping the two combine bodies AS COMPILED INSIDE
probe_dpas9 and identifying them by mangled name:

  combine (split body)      869 lines   mov 158  mul 47  mad 17  cmp.eq 6   or 6  asr 4   3 loads / 1 store
  combine_pair (merged)    1105 lines   mov 229  mul 51  mad 19  cmp.eq 12  or 10 asr 8   3 loads / 1 store

+27 % instructions, concentrated in mov (+71) and the selection/predication ops (cmp.eq, or, asr, shr): the
`PART = a ? PA : PB` select duplicates 64-bit address setup for the two candidate bases, because the base is not
known until run time *inside* the k-slice loop. Memory-op shape is identical, so this is pure address arithmetic
- the LESSONS 49 cost class again.

The tell: in the STANDALONE dumps both schedules reach the second k-slice through a folded immediate
(`flat[V0308+0x64000]`) and the bodies are near-clones. Neither in-situ body has ANY immediate offset - every
address is a computed register - because `KS` is a runtime int in probe_dpas9 while probe_combine.cpp declares
`constexpr int KS = 1`. Identical source loop, different folding, and it lands entirely on the merged form
because the select multiplies the unfolded address math.

Falsifiable prediction: make `KS` a compile-time parameter of the in-situ kernel (D7 already treats it as a
dispatch parameter) and the merged body's +27 % should collapse toward the standalone near-clone, at which point
the merged combine should win in situ by roughly the ~3 us it wins standalone.

Protocol hypothesis, tested and DEAD (anchor interleaved at 19.4 us, healthy, so both columns are trustworthy):

  rows=1    burst-of-8: split 21.50  pair 21.60 (+0.10)    single-launch: split 28.60  pair 28.00 (-0.60)
  rows=8    burst-of-8: split 26.90  pair 28.80 (+1.90)    single-launch: split 33.00  pair 35.80 (+2.80)
  rows=16   burst-of-8: split 34.60  pair 38.10 (+3.50)    single-launch: split 40.80  pair 44.90 (+4.10)

PREDICTION TESTED AND WRONG (same session): making KS a template parameter of both combine bodies (revL3,
anchor 19.4 interleaved, both arms bit-exact) did NOT change the sign or the size of the penalty -
rows=1/8/16 burst: +0.00 / +2.00 / +3.40 us (was +0.10 / +1.90 / +3.50). So the missing immediate offset was
not the cause; the +27 % instruction count of the merged body may still be the cost, but it is not mediated by
KS folding. The templated combine is kept anyway - KS is compile-time-known by D7, and LESSONS 49 stands on
its own evidence (the band case), not on this failed inference.

Ruled out so far for the in-situ inversion: dirty L2 (refuted), measurement protocol / burst depth (refuted),
KS immediate folding (refuted), combine-body memory-op shape (identical 3 loads / 1 store).
Still open, and the next test: partials may not be L2-resident in situ the way they are standalone - the
standalone probe writes 2 x 800 KB once and re-reads it hot, while in situ the fused gemm writes both partial
regions and the layer's other traffic may have evicted them. Test: producer -> scrub a ~32 MB buffer ->
combine, in probe_combine.cpp; if the merged arm loses there, the mechanism is dual-stream DRAM fetch vs
single-stream, and the engine answer is to combine each projection right after its own producer (which argues
against gemm-level fusion of gate+up at rows>1, where E25a already measured the win shrinking). `DPAS_SPLIT` stays in probe_dpas9 as a temporary marker until the eviction test below decides which ships., even though the protocol
is genuinely ~7.8 us at rows=1: the merged launch reads the same partials and writes the same floats, and above
rows=1 it gives up whatever overlap the two smaller grids enjoyed. Neutral at rows=1, monotonically worse with
rows - the shape you expect when the saving is real (one dispatch, fixed) and the loss scales with the work
merged.

Consequence beyond this experiment: **launch-count reduction per se is not a lever on this device.** That
retroactively sharpens E25a: its win came from filling an under-subscribed grid (800 of ~2048 slots at KS=1),
not from launching fewer kernels - which is why fusing at KS=2 cost +7 % instead of helping.

What still stands, and it is not this. The engine-facing version of E25b is a combine that reads both partial
sets, computes silu(gate) * up, and writes ONE product tensor. That is a traffic win (one elementwise pass
eliminated, half the writes) rather than a launch win, and this probe cannot express it: it has no elementwise
stage, and its whole purpose is to check each projection against its own y_ref. It belongs in the
engine-shaped harness where the intermediate feeds the down projection.
