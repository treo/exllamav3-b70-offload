E25b: one combine launch for the whole fused pair - REFUTED
===========================================================
Question. After E25a the layer runs gemm(gate+up fused) -> combine(gate) -> combine(up) -> gemm(down) ->
combine(down). DPAS_FLOOR prices launch+setup+combine at ~7.8 us per projection at rows=1, i.e. more than half
of the rows=1 layer time is fixed cost. E25a showed fusion buys parallelism for the gemm. E25b asked whether it
buys anything for the combine too: merge the pair's two combines into one grid.

Why it looked promising. The two projections are independent, so unlike E29's row bucketing (which had to double
the accumulator set and died on the 128-GRF register file) a work-item still touches exactly one projection. The
extra state is one partial pointer and one output pointer selected by index: register bill flat, one dispatch
saved.

Result (fused gemm unchanged, gate+up on /tmp/gemv_R64 + /tmp/gemv_up_R64, KS=1, min of 5 interleaved rounds,
anchor rev I gate rows=1 = 19.4 us, healthy; both arms bit-exact against each projection's own y_ref, read from
the directory the run wrote):

  rows=1    two combines 21.50 us -> one combine 21.60 us    +0.10 us  (x0.995)
  rows=8    two combines 26.80 us -> one combine 28.70 us    +1.90 us  (x0.934)
  rows=16   two combines 34.60 us -> one combine 38.10 us    +3.50 us  (x0.908)

AMENDED THE SAME SESSION — the conclusion below is RETRACTED. Standalone (probe_combine.cpp, no gemm in the
timed region), the merged combine is ~2x FASTER, not slower: rows=1 2.87 vs split 5.89 us, rows=8 2.86 vs 5.92,
rows=16 3.76 vs 5.81. Split therefore costs ~2.9 us per launch - the protocol cost - and merging removes one of
them, exactly as hypothesized. Both measurements are clean, so the in-situ loss above is an INTERACTION with the
preceding fused gemm (L2 state of freshly written partials, or queue behaviour), not the combine kernel's cost.
"Launch-count reduction per se is not a lever" does not follow from any data here and is withdrawn; the lever is
real in isolation and its in-situ cancellation is an open question. ISA census (asm_e25b/, 4 dumps, 565-994
lines, mov 93-193, mul 27-53, no spill/scratch indications) does not identify which dump is which schedule, so
it adjudicates nothing.

Original reading, kept for the record: A combine's cost is the memory work it performs, not the per-launch protocol, even though the protocol
is genuinely ~7.8 us at rows=1: the merged launch reads the same partials and writes the same floats, and above
rows=1 it gives up whatever overlap the two smaller grids enjoyed. Neutral at rows=1, monotonically worse with
rows - the shape you expect when the saving is real (one dispatch, fixed) and the loss scales with the work
merged.

Consequence beyond this experiment: **launch-count reduction per se is not a lever on this device.** That
retroactively sharpens E25a: its win came from filling an under-subscribed grid (800 of ~2048 slots at KS=1),
not from launching fewer kernels - which is why fusing at KS=2 cost +7 % instead of helping.

What still stands, and it is not this. The engine-facing version of E25b is a combine that reads both partial
sets, computes silu(gate) * up, and writes ONE product tensor. That is a traffic win (one elementwise pass
eliminated, half the writes) rather than a launch win, and this probe cannot express it: it has no elementwise
stage, and its whole purpose is to check each projection against its own y_ref. It belongs in the
engine-shaped harness where the intermediate feeds the down projection.
