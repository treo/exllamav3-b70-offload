Sustained decode load: 150 s soak, power at cap, no performance drift
======================================================================
The standing gap: every rate in this chapter is a warm-window number (continuous work inside the timing process,
min-of-N), never a soak. Question: does sustained decode drift - thermally, or by hitting a power limit?

Method. `WARM_S` is the soak knob now that the warm-up loop waits every 8 dispatches (LESSONS 53): it executes
real device work for the given wall time, and the timing loops run AFTER it - so the reported number is measured
deep inside continuous load. One process, 150 s of continuous fused gate+up decode at E=96, rows=16, KS=2 (2 gemm
launches + 1 combine_both per iteration, ~412 us/iteration, i.e. roughly 360k layer-iterations), while a sampler
polled `xpu-smi stats -d 0 -j` every ~4 s (35 samples over 146 s). Probe exit rc=0.

RESULT - performance:
  warm 0 s: 404.3 us | warm 1 s: 410.1 | warm 2-3 s: 409.9 | AFTER 150 s SOAK: 412.1 us (single-launch 417.x)
  drift vs the 1-3 s warm window: +0.5%. Nothing in the chapter's decode numbers is a soak artifact.

RESULT - the device is POWER-LIMITED under decode:

  metric (tile_0)                     first    mean     max    last
  gpu_power_w                          56      285     294     290      cap reported by xpu-smi: 275 W
  gpu_frequency_mhz                   1200    2393    2458    2416      (1200 = the idle first sample; the early
                                                                        peak 2458 vs late 2433 max is ~1% droop)
  compute_percent                        0       97     100     100
  memory bandwidth_percent               0       54     100     100
  memory read_kbps                       -    3.78e8  7.08e8  7.05e8   = 378 GB/s avg, 708 GB/s peak
  memory write_kbps                      -    1.00e8  1.89e8  1.86e8   = 100 GB/s avg
  gpu_core_celsius                      51       70      71      71     plateau by ~60 s
  memory_celsius                        54       80      86      86     STILL RISING at the end of 150 s
  vr_celsius                            67       90      94      94     hottest component, still rising
  memory used_mib                      231      650     662     662

Reading it:
  * Idle ~56 W, sustained decode ~285 W average with 294-306 W peaks, against a 275 W reported cap. Average
    demand exceeds the cap, so the limit is engaged: frequency sits at ~2.4 GHz with ~1% droop from its early
    peak, and that is exactly the magnitude of variation the anchor has been showing. Numbers in this chapter
    that differ by 1-2% are inside the power-cap envelope, not evidence of anything.
  * Compute units are saturated (97-100%) while DRAM bandwidth averages ~54% of peak. So the adopted kernel at
    E=96/rows=16 is limited by issue/arithmetic+power, not by bytes - consistent with the CPU-home story being
    the reverse (bandwidth-bound) and with the quant ladder (out_quant_ladder.txt) showing decode TIME insensitive
    to bits-per-weight at small E.
  * Steady state for the core is reached (~71 C) but memory (86 C) and VR (94 C) temps were still climbing at
    150 s. 150 s is not a long soak. For anything hour-scale the VR stage is the component to watch, and this box
    is a workstation card in a desktop chassis - thermal results here should not be transferred to a dense
    deployment without a real long run.

Planning consequences:
  1. Power per decode layer, E=96 gate+up at rows=16: ~412 us at ~290 W board power. Energy per layer-iteration
     ~120 uJ. Extrapolated over 48 MoE layers x tokens/s this is a real share of the box's budget, and it is a
     cap-limited share: reducing kernel TIME without reducing ENERGY does not escape the cap, so power-aware
     optimisation should target work eliminated (launches, unpack instructions, redundant reads), not just
     latency-hiding.
  2. Because the part is at its cap, the "spare bandwidth" argument in the cost model should be read as spare
     bandwidth *at the expense of clock*: bytes moved and GHz are trading against each other here, which is the
     mechanism behind the flat quant ladder.
  3. Clock state dominates everything else on this box: the device idles at 1200 MHz and takes ~40 ms of
     continuous work to leave it, which is a 2.2-2.3x effect on any measured time - larger than most things this
     chapter measures. Check frequency (xpu-smi) before attributing a slow reading to contention or to heat. The
     co-tenant is real (firefox holds the render node, LESSONS 54) and shares the same 275 W, but it has not been
     shown to have affected any batch here.

CAVEATS: single config, single card, one desktop box, no pinned clocks, co-tenant present (the memory counters
include anything else using the device). The +0.5% soak drift is the ratio that matters; the absolute 412.1 us
was recorded in a window whose anchor initially read 23.8 us, though that turned out to be an under-warmed anchor
rather than a slow device (LESSONS 54), so the absolutes are likely fine - re-verify against a 6000-rep anchor
before quoting them as certified.

Reproduce:
    # terminal 1: soak; WARM_S is the soak duration, the timed rounds run after it
    WARM_S=150 /tmp/revL9 /tmp/f96_gate 3 16 2 native /tmp/f96_up
    # terminal 2: sample
    while sleep 4; do xpu-smi stats -d 0 -j; done > /tmp/stats.jsonl
    # useful paths: power.gpu_power_w.tile_0.{avg,max}, frequency.gpu_frequency_mhz.tile_0.avg,
    #               memory.bandwidth_percent.tile_0.avg, temperature.{gpu_core,memory,vr}_celsius.tile_0.*,
    #               utilization.compute_percent.tile_0
    xpu-smi            # one-shot table: temp, Pwr:Usage/Cap, memory, GPU-Util
