Per-KS launch table: the joint (fusion, split-K) rule, measured over E = 2..96
================================================================================
Closes the follow-up left by out_low_e_grid.txt: plan_decode_cost collapsed split-K inside its tables, so it
could not express the joint choice that the low-E sweep implied. Instrument: notes/b70/sweep_ks_grid.py (kept in
the repo; it prints per-expert cost for both schedules at every (E, rows, KS) and carries the anchor between
cells).

CERTIFIED WINDOW: the anchor (rev I gate rows=1, run with 6000 reps so it ramps its own clock - LESSONS 54) read
19.0-19.3 us on all 27 cells of the main sweep and 19.1-19.6 us on the exactness passes. Unlike some earlier
batches in this directory, these absolute numbers are quotable.
EXACTNESS: every fused cell checked here is bit-exact for BOTH projections, including KS=4 (eight partial slices
per output) and KS=8. The first pass reported failures that were a bug in the check itself - comparing a flat file
read against a 3-D reference without reshaping broadcasts and raises ValueError, which read as a correctness
failure (fixed in the script).

THE RULE - AFTER RETRACTING MY OWN FIRST VERSION OF IT
-------------------------------------------------------
I first wrote "at equal occupancy fusion always wins, by a constant 1.13x". That was an artifact of matching the
wrong pairs: I compared separate KS=2 against fused KS=1 because their PER-LAUNCH thread counts coincide (5120 at
E=32). But separate runs two launches, so at equal per-launch grid the fused option is doing twice the k-work per
thread in half the launches - both levers moved at once, which is exactly the confound this chapter exists to
control. Holding KS fixed makes the total thread-slots equal by construction (separate 2 x tn*KS*2E, fused
1 x 2tn*KS*2E):

  fuse gain at matched KS, rows=1:     KS=1     KS=2     KS=4
     E=2                               1.87x    1.70x    1.48x
     E=4                               1.82x    1.68x    1.01x
     E=10                              1.62x    0.99x    1.12x
     E=32                              0.96x    0.79x       -
     E=96                              1.00x    1.06x       -

1. Fusion's benefit IS the benefit of concurrency and nothing more. 1.6-1.9x while the separate launches leave
   slots empty; gone (0.96-1.12x) once they do not. That is D8 as written, now with KS measured instead of
   assumed - and the low-E half of the earlier finding stands on its own: at E=2/4 the best schedule is fused AND
   KS=4 / KS=2, because neither lever alone fills the device.

2. Best schedule per (E, rows), read straight off the tables (us per expert+pair):
     E=2   rows 1/8/16: fused KS=4     4.750 / 5.350 / 5.700   (grid 1280 = 62.5% of HW)
     E=4   rows 1/8/16: fused KS=2     3.025 / 3.625 / 4.375   (1280 = 62.5%)
     E=10  rows 1/8/16: fused KS=1     2.080 / 2.630 / 3.370   (1600 = 78.1%)
     E=32  rows 1/8/16: separate KS=2  2.010 / 2.896 / 3.588   (5120/launch = 250%)
     E=96  rows 1/8/16: fused KS=1     2.507 / 3.050 / 3.765   (separate KS=1 within 0.5%)
   Where filling is needed (E=2/4/10) the optimum lands at ~0.6-0.8 x HW_THREADS, as the rule says. Past ~1 wave
   the schedules converge to within a few percent - EXCEPT E=32 rows=1, where separate KS=2 beats every fused
   option by 27% at matched KS (2.010 vs 2.544). UNEXPLAINED, and too large to dismiss as high-fill noise: at
   KS=2 the fused grid is 10240 threads (5 waves) against 5120 per launch (2.5 waves) for the same total work, so
   a wave-quantization effect from the doubled tile count is the obvious suspect - a hypothesis, not a result. The
   follow-up is one cell of data. Operationally it changes nothing: D8 already says do not fuse at E=32.

3. Cost-model correction: fused per-pair cost at E=10 rows=1 is 2.080 us against the 2.57 DPAS_FUSE_GU has
   carried since E25a; rows=8 2.630 (was 2.87), rows=16 3.370 (was 3.50). The old figures predate E25b's
   combine_both, when the fused schedule still paid two combines, so planners using them overstate B70 fused cost
   by up to 19% at rows=1. Refreshed in plan_decode_cost.py, together with the KS-keyed tables and a chooser.

THE E=32 "ANOMALY" IS NOT AN ANOMALY - it is L2, and the rule needs a footprint term
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The E=32 cell where separate beat fused by 1.27x reproduced to within 1% across two independent re-runs (sep
KS=2 2.006 vs fused KS=2 2.528; sep KS=1 2.504 vs fused KS=1 2.61), so it was real - and E=16 and E=64 did NOT
show it, which made it non-monotonic in E and immune to any occupancy explanation. Filling the gap (E=16/20/24/64
measured at rows=1, all bit-exact, anchors 19.1-19.3):

  E    matched-KS fused gain   fused footprint 2*E*0.585 MiB    single-launch footprint E*0.585 MiB
  10   1.62x  (fused)                  11.7 MiB   < L2                    5.9 MiB
  16   1.29x  (fused, at KS=2)         18.7 MiB   < L2                   18.8 MiB
  20   0.84x  (SEPARATE wins)          23.4 MiB  ~= L2 (24 MiB)          11.7 MiB
  24   0.81x  (SEPARATE wins)          28.1 MiB   > L2                   14.0 MiB
  32   0.79x  (SEPARATE wins)          37.4 MiB   > L2                   18.8 MiB  <- each stream still fits
  64   1.05x  (fused, marginally)      74.9 MiB   > L2                   37.4 MiB  <- neither fits
  96   1.00x  (indifferent)           112.3 MiB   > L2                   56.2 MiB  <- neither fits

One line explains the whole curve: **a fused launch's instantaneous weight footprint is the UNION of both
projections' streams, while two separate launches each hold one stream.** With L2 = 24 MiB and 0.585 MiB of gate
trellis per expert here:
  * 2*E*b <= L2 (E <= ~20): fused enjoys cache reuse over both streams and wins on top of its fill advantage.
  * E*b <= L2 < 2*E*b (E ~ 20-41): the penalty band. Separate keeps each stream cache-resident while fused
    thrashes - worth up to 1.27x, and big enough to overturn a fusion decision made on occupancy alone.
  * E*b > L2 (E > ~41): neither shape is cached, and the levers wash (+-6%) - which is exactly E=64/96.

Restated D8, two conditions instead of one: fuse gate+up when (a) the separate launches do not fill the device -
the occupancy term, unchanged - AND (b) the fused footprint stays under L2. Inside the penalty band prefer
separate launches even though fusion would fill better; above the band the choice does not matter. Both terms are
computable from what the engine already knows (tn, KS, E, bytes per expert, L2 size).

Untested predictions this makes: the band should move with quantization (K=6 at 1.17 MiB/expert puts it at
E ~ 10-20) and should narrow as rows grows (more compute per byte, less cache leverage). Each is a couple of runs
of sweep_ks_grid.py.

PREDICTION TESTED (same day, independent checkpoint) - crossover CONFIRMED, magnitude NOT explained
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The band claim says the fusing regime is set by BYTES, not by E alone, so switching to the 6.05 bpw recipe
(b = 1.17 MiB/expert for gate) should move the crossover from E ~ 20 to E = L2/(2b) = 24/2.34 = 10.3 - the SAME
E=10 that fused 1.62x better at K=3 should stop paying. Measured at K=6, rows=1, anchors 19.1-19.2 us, every
fused cell bit-exact for both projections:

   E   matched KS=1: separate/pair   fused/pair   gain          2*E*b (L2 = 24 MiB)
  10          3.550                  2.830      1.254x fused         23.4 MiB  ~= L2
  16          2.650                  4.731      0.560x  SEPARATE by 1.79x      37.4 MiB  > L2
   E   matched KS=2:
  10          2.700                  2.870      0.941x  SEPARATE (fusion already losing at E=10)
  16          3.332                  4.419      0.754x  SEPARATE

Sign and location: CONFIRMED. Fused gain at E=10 KS=1 fell from 1.62x (K=3) to 1.25x (K=6); by KS=2 fusion was
already losing at E=10; and at E=16 - where K=3 still preferred fusion by 1.29x - K=6 loses by 1.79x. The crossover
landed between E=10 and E=16 against a predicted E ~ 10.3, on a different checkpoint, with nothing refitted.

Magnitude: NOT explained. At the same absolute live footprint (37.4 MiB) the K=6/E=16 penalty is 1.79x while the
K=3/E=32 penalty was 1.27x. So 2*E*b vs L2 predicts WHEN fusion turns bad, not HOW bad; the remainder needs a term
the simple model omits - most plausibly that a miss at K=6 exposes more uncovered arithmetic per byte (denser
unpack), so latency costs more. Testable by holding 2*E*b fixed and sweeping K = 2..6 with E inversely proportional
to b, which would turn the rule from directional to quantitative. Until then: the L2 test is the switch, the
measured tables are the size.

Rows dependence: still untested - the runs above are rows=1. "The band narrows as rows grows" remains a
prediction, not a result.

FOLLOW-UP ANSWERED (`out_fusion_penalty_k.txt`): the fixed-footprint K sweep was run, and it resolved the
magnitude question - penalty depth scales with bits-per-weight (worst case 1.16x / 1.27x / 1.88x at K=2/3/6,
sub-linear in traffic) - but it ALSO refuted the byte test as a decision rule: K=2 at E=32 lies inside the
predicted band and fusion won there by 1.32x, and K=3 at E=16 flips sign between KS=1 and KS=2 at identical
bytes. `best_launch()` therefore treats the L2 arithmetic as a warning and decides from measured cells.

This is LESSONS 55 (never quote a rate without its working-set size) paying rent a second time: the curve looked
non-monotonic only because one of the two shapes' footprints was being ignored.

LIMITS OF THIS TABLE
  * E coverage is now 2, 4, 10, 16, 20, 24, 32, 64, 96. The model tables (KS_SEP / KS_FUSED) carry the
    2/4/10/32/96 cells over rows 1/8/16; the E=16/20/24/64 cells are rows=1 only and live here, because their
    purpose was locating the fusion penalty band rather than populating the model.
  * E is experts-in-launch (the grid axis) - what the planners call `picks` when all owned experts run here.
    Fractional ownership means interpolating on E, which the chooser refuses to do silently: nearest measured E,
    flagged.
  * K=3 for the model tables. K=6 was measured at rows=1, E=10/16 to test the band claim (section above), so a
    K axis in KS_SEP/KS_FUSED is now backed by data at two points - but the planners still run K=3 tables and
    should not be assumed quantitative for the dense recipes.
  * KS is limited by tk/KS needing even halves (tk=160 allows up to 20; only 1/2/4/8 were run).
  * down_proj keeps its measured factor (DPAS_TRANSPOSED_DOWN_FACTOR) applied to the chosen single-projection cost.
