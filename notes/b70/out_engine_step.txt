The parent's real per-layer decode cost, measured inside the engine - and the 83 us/layer nobody modelled
=============================================================================================================
P1 of the plan's remaining list. Two numbers claimed to be the 3090's non-expert cost per MoE layer at b=1:
**122 us**, composed bottom-up from separate microbenches (out_parent_leg.txt), and **257 us**, what the planner
gets after calibrating a `fixed` overhead so the parent bill reproduces the inherited ~250 us/layer window.
LESSONS 61 recorded that single-stream t/s differs 2.1x depending on which is used, and that no further
micro-benchmarking could settle it, because the disputed part was precisely what the floor declined to model.

It was settled by measuring the engine's own step. Instruments (both new, both in-tree):
  notes/bench_engine_step.py  - times `model.forward` decode at b=1 and buckets DEVICE time by kernel name, so the
                                parent's own work separates from whatever runs the experts.
  notes/bench_live_trace.py   - the real generator (prefill + greedy decode of real text), per-token expert sets,
                                placement-migration counters.
  notes/b70/run_home_matrix.sh, notes/b70/run_mcpa_arms.sh, notes/b70/run_mcpa_migration.sh - the arm scripts.
Checkpoint Qwen3.8@2.05 / @2.68 with `--moe_cpu_split` (mandatory: these checkpoints do not fit in 24 GiB), ctx
4096, b=1. Control: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B@3bpw, which fits entirely on the GPU, so it has no CPU leg to obscure things.

1. THE DECOMPOSITION (device time, us per layer; `expert` = routed-expert + split/handoff kernels)

  model / config                              wall    device  expert   gemm  hyperconn  attn  linstate  norm
  Qwen3.5@3bpw, every expert on the GPU       222.0    179.7    75.5     73.0      7.5     7.0      7.2    9.4
  Qwen3.5@3bpw, -mcs 128 -mcpa                227.3    187.1    79.0     71.1      ~7      ~7       ~7     ~9
  Qwen3.8@2.05, -mcs 256                      375.4    267.2   125.3     40.6     81.8     3.3      9.7    5.9
  Qwen3.8@2.68, -mcs 384                      634.2    284.8    89.2     93.0     82.8     3.4      9.8    5.9

  **Parent non-expert device work = device - expert = 141.9 us/layer (2.05), 195.6 us/layer (2.68), 104.2 for the
  Qwen3.5 control.** These are routing-independent: the non-expert kernels do not care which experts were picked,
  which is why passing synthetic token ids (bench_engine_step's input) does not bias section 1's numbers - see
  section 4 for where that shortcut DID bias a conclusion.

  Cross-window warning, measured: re-running the identical Qwen3.5 all-GPU config later gave wall 182.6 -> 222.0
  us/layer (22%) while its device total moved only 186.9 -> 179.7 (4%). On this box the engine-step WALL is not
  comparable across windows (shared render node, host contention); the DEVICE bucket sums are. Quote device
  figures, or quote wall with its window.

2. THE 83 us/LAYER THAT NO MODEL IN THE PLAN CONTAINED
   The `other` bucket is 81.8-82.8 us/layer on Qwen3.8 (7.5 on Qwen3.5, which does not use the feature). It is one
   thing: **hyper-connections**. The kernels are `gr_dots_kernel2` (26.7 us/layer), `gr_finalize_kernel2` (28.6)
   and `hc_apply_kernel` (4.9) from exllamav3/exllamav3_ext/hc_mix.cu + modules/hyperconnections.py - the
   `hc_mult` residual-stream machinery. Neither the bottom-up floor (attention + streamed weights + recurrent
   state) nor the planner's byte model has a term for it. It is ~42% of the parent's non-expert work on 2.05 and
   ~43% on 2.68, and at ~14 us per call for tens of KB of fp32 streams per token it is nowhere near
   bandwidth-bound - the calls are simply expensive at b=1. Two consequences: a bytes-per-layer prediction for
   this architecture will be ~80 us/layer low in a component that **offloading experts cannot touch**, and the
   single-stream ceiling is set by it (see section 6).

3. RECONCILIATION (Qwen3.8@2.68, b=1, ctx 4096)
     bottom-up floor         122 us/layer  = 0.62x the measured 195.6. Missing hyper-connections (~83) and light
                                           on the dense projections; meanwhile its ATTENTION term was too high -
                                           the engine's own attention averages 3.4 us/layer over the 48 layers,
                                           where the SDPA proxy used in the floor said 9.9 (3x pessimistic). The
                                           errors partly cancelled, which is why the floor looked respectable.
     window-calibrated       259 us/layer  = 1.32x the parent's own measured work, i.e. too high as a model of
                                           what the parent must do - yet too LOW as a model of what a step costs
                                           today: the real generator on real text measures 357.1 us/layer
                                           (58.3 tok/s) with sampler and lm_head included.
     measured (this file)    195.6 us/layer of parent non-expert device work; 357.1 us/layer end to end today.
   So the plan was wrong in both directions at once: it over-charged the parent's *obligatory* work and
   under-charged the parent's *actual* step. Both errors flattered the CPU home and understated the case for an
   asynchronous worker, which is what sections 4-6 quantify.

4. CAVEAT THAT CHANGED A CONCLUSION MID-FLIGHT: SYNTHETIC INPUTS ARE NOT NEUTRAL FOR EVERY KERNEL
   bench_engine_step.py feeds random token ids. Device buckets are unaffected (section 1 stands), but WALL time
   is not: uniform-random routing spreads picks over all 512 experts, so with `-mcs 384` about 75% of the picks
   land on the CPU and the measured wall (634 us/layer) is dominated by that leg. Real text routes to the ~24
   experts holding half the picks, most of them GPU-resident, and the same model with the same split measures
   357.1 us/layer. The "349 us/layer of exposed stall" I first read out of (wall - device) was therefore an
   artifact of the input distribution, not a property of the shipping path. A synthetic input is only neutral for
   the kernels that ignore it - routing is not one of them.

5. TODAY'S SHIPPING BASELINE, AND WHAT WARMING IT IS WORTH
   Real text, real generator, two passes through one placement-profile directory (Qwen3.8, -mct 15, ctx 4096):
     2.68, -mcs 384:  pass 1  649.4 us/layer (32.1 tok/s)   ->  pass 2  357.1 us/layer (58.3 tok/s)
     2.05, -mcs 256:  pass 1  388.7 us/layer (53.6 tok/s)   ->  pass 2  290.2 us/layer (71.8 tok/s)
   **Dynamic placement learning is worth 1.6-1.8x on decode.** That is the plan's "churn itself unmeasured and
   unmeasurable offline" row, measured: churn is observable, it is large, and any A/B of worker designs that does
   not equalise profile warmth is comparing warm-up curves, not designs. (Architecture section 12 predicted the
   row was unmeasurable offline; it is measurable online with two passes, which is the cheap way.)

   Pinned arena (`-mcpa`) is the clearest lesson in this file, because my first read of it was wrong in a
   instructive way. Over 192-token passes it looks clearly harmful:
     2.05: 71.8 -> 63.0 tok/s      2.68: 58.3 -> 48.8      Qwen3.5: 126.9 -> 82.1 (reproducing the recorded
                                                              acceptance figure ~100 -> ~78, port-acceptance E6)
   So I wrote "-mcpa costs decode 12-35%" and moved on. Doubling the horizon and reading the ENGINE'S OWN
   migration counters instead of only the token rate (notes/b70/run_mcpa_migration.sh) inverts the conclusion:
     plain  -mcs 256, 384 tokens:  0 promotions CPU->GPU, 0 demotions, 10 sweeps -> 50.0 then 64.5 tok/s
     -mcpa  -mcs 256, 384 tokens:  3,689 promotions (pass 1), 1,194 (pass 2), 796 sweeps -> 53.2 then 64.6 tok/s
   **Without the pinned arena hot experts cannot migrate to the GPU at all - measured zero promotions across 48
   layers - and with it thousands move.** The throughput penalty is the price of that mobility and it is paid up
   front: by pass 2 (384 tokens) the two arms are equal (64.5 vs 64.6). A 192-token A/B publishes the cost and
   misses the benefit, because the mechanism's latency is longer than the comparison.
   Two consequences. (1) For the plan: any worker design, CPU or B70, is judged wrongly if the arm is shorter
   than the placement adaptation time - equalise warmth AND horizon. (2) For the trace instrument: a live
   permutation makes the snapshot-diff trace attribute picks to the wrong experts (diversity inflated - visible
   as "half of picks" jumping from 23.8 to 42.0 in the same config), so bench_live_trace.py now counts permutation
   changes and refuses to stay quiet about them; every trace quoted in out_live_trace.txt came from a plain (non
   `-mcpa`) arm where the permutation provably never moved.

6. SYNCHRONIZATION IS THE EXPENSIVE PART - AND IT HAS A PRICE PER DELEGATED SHARD
   Qwen3.8@2.68, same process, same tokens:
     generator, pipelined (expert work overlaps later layers):  359.7 us/layer
     model.forward, synchronized per token:                     542.8 us/layer  -> +183.1 us/layer
   Qwen3.8@2.05 (half as much delegated): 291.8 -> 336.0, i.e. +44.3 us/layer. So the cost of waiting scales with
   how much is delegated, and at the delegation fraction a capacity-starved config actually uses it is enormous.
   This prices the architecture's "completion by pinned flag, never .wait()" rule ~25x above the 24 us-vs-5-8 us
   round-trip comparison it was originally justified with: a B70 engine that joined per token would hand back more
   than the card saves, because the B70's whole expert leg for the same layer is 36-45 us/layer.
   Adjacent control on the CPU home: with the worker pinned to 16 logical processors, raising `-mct` from 16 to 32
   moved 2.05 real-text decode from 315.5 to 574.3 us/layer (1.82x WORSE). The right setting is below the pin set
   (15 on this box, leaving one core for GPU<->CPU traffic), and any knob that scales threads must be clamped to
   the LP allocation rather than defaulted to nproc.

7. THE CEILING THIS IMPLIES (and what to attack next)
   With experts on a B70 and the parent doing only its own measured work, b=1 decode is bounded by
   parent non-expert + head/sampler: 2.05 141.9 + ~14 -> ~133 tok/s, 2.68 195.6 + ~14 -> ~100 tok/s, against
   today's measured 71.8 and 58.3 -> a **1.4-1.8x single-stream win that does not depend on any B70 speed
   assumption at all** (the card is 3-5x below the binding leg at b=1). Beyond that win, the next lever is not
   the card and not the experts: it is `hc_mix.cu`, whose ~83 us/layer is the largest single non-expert item on
   this architecture, followed by the dense `exl3_mgemm` projections (40.6-93.0 us/layer, which the 2.68 recipe
   pays nearly 2.3x more of than 2.05 - a quantisation effect on the NON-expert weights worth its own probe).
   The head/sampler figure (~14 us/layer equivalent) is from the Qwen3.5 control only: for Qwen3.8 the two-pass
   subtraction is contaminated by pipelining (section 6), which is why the control model exists.

8. MATCHED-BATCH BASELINE: TODAY'S CPU PATH DOES NOT SCALE WITH CONCURRENCY
   `bench_live_trace.py --jobs N` runs N sessions through one generator (distinct prompt windows; the instrument
   refuses identical sessions because they would measure union(1) and call it union(N)). Qwen3.8@2.68, -mcs 384,
   -mct 15, real text, aggregate tokens/s over all sessions:
        b=1        b=2        b=4        b=8       b=16
      58.3       33.2       33.3       32.5       33.1   tok/s
   Throughput FALLS 43% going from one session to two and then is flat to b=16: batch buys nothing on this path.
   The routing measurement says why the work grows (union per layer 9.9 -> 18.2 across two sessions -> 45.9
   across six, out_live_trace.txt section 8) while the home that serves it cannot - 15 CPU threads are already the
   constraint at b=1. Two caveats kept visible: this is one split size (384 of 512, the smallest that loads in
   24 GiB) and one box, and the acceptance-era figure of ~100 tok/s was a shallower split on a model whose experts
   fit; it is NOT evidence that a better-tuned CPU split scales, only that this one - the one a 36 GiB expert set
   is forced into - does not. It is also why out_throughput_report.txt can now quote gains at matched batch.
   (The engine steps sequences one at a time, so these numbers are aggregate throughput, not a per-step batching
   study; see out_live_trace.txt section 9.)

9. STILL UNMEASURED
   * ctx dependence of the parent term (everything here is ctx 4096). The components argue for weak dependence -
     engine attention is 3.4 us/layer at 4k - but "argue" is not "measure".
   * b > 1 device decomposition. Matched-batch THROUGHPUT baselines are measured now (section 8); what is not
     measured is the kernel bucket split at b>1, so the planner's engine mode borrows the calibrated model's
     SHAPE and re-anchors its LEVEL to these b=1 measurements (`parent_engine_us`), marking every such cell `^`.
   * GLM's parent leg (42 layers, 9 MiB/expert, no measurement anywhere).
   * The 4.05/5.05/6.05 baselines in PARENT_TODAY_US are single-pass at -mcs 448 (VRAM-forced, 64 experts on the
     GPU). They are NOT tuned CPU-path numbers and must never be used as the denominator of a B70 gain claim;
     that is recorded in plan_parent_budget.py's own comment and repeated in out_throughput_report.txt.

Reproduce:
    source notes/env.sh
    python3 notes/bench_engine_step.py -m <ckpt> -mcs 256 -mct 15 -cs 8192 --ctx 4096 --tokens 48
    python3 notes/bench_live_trace.py  -m <ckpt> -mcs 256 -mct 15 --decode 192 --out /tmp/trace.json
    bash notes/b70/run_home_matrix.sh          # arms x 2 passes; -mct 15
    bash notes/b70/run_mcpa_arms.sh            # -mcpa arms + the Qwen3.5 device buckets
    bash notes/b70/run_mcpa_migration.sh       # promotion/demotion counters
  Splits below ~256 (2.05) / ~384 (2.68) / ~448 (4.05+) fail with "Insufficient VRAM in split for model and
  cache": the split is chosen by what fits, which is exactly why section 8's last bullet exists.
