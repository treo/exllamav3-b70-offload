=== READOUT ================================================================================
Per expert-projection bucket at rows=64, ks=2 (E=64, so divide by 64):

    variant    us total    us/bucket   G-w/s    GB/s(trellis)
    A sequential  2389.9     37.34     224.6      84.2

1. CO-SCHEDULING WINS AT GLM GEOMETRY, by 12.7 %. D17's rule was established on Qwen shapes only and
   slab_advice() explicitly refused to answer for glm ("do not borrow Qwen's sign", LESSONS 85). It can answer now:
   E=64 x 2 x 1.5 MiB = 192 MiB = 8x L2, and the re-use story predicts a win there -- measured 0.873, sitting
   between the Qwen E=128/256 rungs (0.94-0.95) and E=512 (0.85-0.91), consistent with the operative variable
   being weights-overflowing-L2 rather than E itself.
2. THE BORROWED RATE WAS OPTIMISTIC. Tiering 5's GLM rows priced the card at 132 GB/s, a decode-shaped constant.
   At the prefill shape GLM actually has, the card sustains 84-97 GB/s. GLM's cost per bucket is 37.3 us against
   Qwen's 7.2 at K=2 -- 5.2x for 7.7x the bytes, so still sublinear, but the absolute rate is ~35 % below what
   those rows assumed. Any GLM split or throughput figure should be re-derived at ~85-97 GB/s (or better, at
   32.6-37.3 us per bucket, which is the primitive D18 asks for).
3. AND IT BREAKS THE SINGLE LINE. Plotting all four measured shapes (Qwen K=2/4/6 and GLM K=3) against bytes per
   bucket gives marginal rates of ~172 GB/s within the Qwen family and ~87 GB/s for GLM -- there is no universal
   fixed+slope*K law. The honest primitive is per (geometry, K, rows), and the fit in out_prefill_kscale.txt is
   valid only inside Qwen3.8's shape.
