Parent leg measured on the 3090 - the kernel is the bottleneck, not the bandwidth (and the box has the GPU all along)
======================================================================================================================
The chapter's last un-measured input: `T_parent`, the 3090's own per-layer decode time. It entered
plan_parent_budget.py as an inherited constant (t0 = 250 us/layer at b=1), the stream leg used rate_g = 936 GB/s,
and the open-items row said this needed "a CUDA box". It did not: the 3090 is in this machine (PCI 0c:00.0), the
extension is built with CUDA, and tests/testlib/moe.py already assembles the real fused-MoE kernel's argument
list. notes/bench_parent_leg.py measures the legs; two full runs are reported side by side.

Shapes are the real ones (Qwen3.8@2.68: H=2560, I=640, topk=10, 48 layers = 12 full + 36 linear attention, 24 q
heads / 2 kv heads, head_dim 256, 512 experts). The expert leg calls **ext.exl3_moe itself** - the same fused
kernel the engine dispatches for small batches (`block_sparse_mlp.py:1198` uses it while `num_tokens*topk <=
fused_rows`, and MOE_FUSED_ROWS/FUSED_ROWS_WIDE are 128/256, so every b measured here is inside that window).
Expert *bytes* are synthetic (rand_experts at K=3); shapes, counts and the kernel are not.

1. STREAMED READS: the assumed 936 GB/s is the datasheet, not the achievable figure
   best-of-7 runs: 859 and 864 GB/s across two invocations; individual samples 639-864 (a 35% spread, so the
   weak samples are the usual un-warmed/contended-device story, and only the best-of-N is quotable). Use ~860.

2. THE PARENT'S EXPERT LEG IS NOT BANDWIDTH-BOUND AT ALL - it runs at ~170-215 GB/s at every batch measured:

    b   rows A  distinct exp | expert us (run1/run2) | implied GB/s | what bytes/rate_g charged | ratio
    1      10        10       |    103.3 /  90.0      |  179 / 205   |        21.5 / 21.3      |  4.8x / 4.2x
    2      20        20       |    171.6 / 171.9      |  215 / 214   |        42.9 / 42.7      |  4.0x / 4.0x
    4      40        39       |    361.8 / 412.9      |  199 / 174   |        83.7 / 83.2      |  4.3x / 5.0x
    8      80        73       |    690.5 / 787.2      |  195 / 171   |       156.7 / 155.7     |  4.4x / 5.1x
   16     160       131       |   1163.7 / 1396.8     |  207 / 173   |       281.2 / 279.4     |  4.1x / 5.0x

   The implied rate is FLAT in b, which is the signature of a kernel-bound (not bandwidth-bound) leg: batching
   buys the parent nothing in this range. Every model that charges the parent's expert work as bytes/936 is
   understating it by roughly 4-5x. This is the single biggest correction in this file.

3. ATTENTION (decode: one query row against cached KV), one full_attention layer, SDPA fp16 - a PROXY for the
   engine's own kernel, and a pessimistic one at small KV where launch dominates:

     b  ctx 4096 (us/layer)      ctx 16384          implied KV bandwidth
     1     39.6 / 53.9           83.0 / 83.9        156-212 GB/s  (launch-bound: 8 MiB of KV in 40-54 us)
     2     49.4 / 51.1          136.4 / 132.3       328-340
     4     68.0 / 83.1          217.2 / 197.9       404-493
     8    105.5 / 105.0         391.2 / 427.4       628-639
    16    177.4 / 201.0         710.2 / 723.5       668-757
   Cost is linear in b·ctx (as it must be: KV bytes), and only approaches streaming rates once the KV working
   set is big. Averaged over the real 12-of-48 layer mix that is 10-50 us/layer at 4k context.

4. COMPOSED per-layer parent time, and the credibility check against the inherited 250 us:

     b   run1 / run2 (us/layer, ctx=4096)     expert   attn   stream  linstate
     1      224.2 / 213.8                     90-103   10-14   105     5.5
     2      300.4 / 300.4                    172       12      105    11
     4      506.3 / 560.4                    362-413   17-21   105    22
     8      866.3 / 962.0                    690-787   26      105    44
    16     1401.5 / 1639.2                  1164-1397  44-50   105    88

   At b=1 the bottom-up composition lands 12-15% BELOW the inherited 250 us - close enough to trust the
   decomposition (and the gap is expected: sampler/lm_head, graph overheads and the rest of the network are not
   modelled, so this is a floor). The headline is the SHAPE: the parent does not stay at ~250 us as batch grows,
   it goes to ~1.4-1.6 ms/layer at b=16, and essentially all of that growth is the expert leg, not attention.

CONSEQUENCES FOR THE PLANNER (applied in plan_parent_budget.py)
  * rate_g 936 -> 860 for streamed non-expert bytes (measured best-of-N).
  * The parent's own expert work is charged at the measured kernel rate (~190 GB/s, the midpoint of the flat
    170-215 band) instead of a byte rate. Experts left on the parent are ~4.7x more expensive than the old model.
  * T_parent stops being a constant: the non-expert floor is ~120 us/layer at ctx=4096 and grows with b·ctx
    through attention and linear-state traffic; a measured table is used, with nearest-neighbour lookup and a
    refusal to extrapolate past b=16 / ctx=16384.
  * Direction of the placement conclusion is unchanged but sharpened: at b=1 the parent still binds (~120 us of
    non-expert work vs ~36 us for the B70 to run the same layer's experts at E=10 rows=1: 20.8 us fused gate+up
    plus ~15 us down), and it binds because of attention + streamed non-expert weights - NOT because of experts.
    Offloading experts helps the parent far more than the old model credited, because its expert leg was being
    undervalued by ~4.7x.

WHAT IS NOT MEASURED HERE (stated, not smoothed)
  * Synthetic expert BYTES (random trellis at K=3): shapes, counts and the kernel are real; the quantization mix
    of the checkpoint (which is a 2.68 bpw blend) is not. A real-checkpoint run would move the number somewhat.
  * Attention is SDPA, not the engine's kernel; the linear-attention leg is pure state traffic (no arithmetic),
    so both are bounds rather than measurements - attention pessimistic at small b, linstate optimistic.
  * No sampler/lm_head/graph/network overhead -> composed figures are floors.
  * One 3090, one desktop box, samples varying 639-864 GB/s; only best-of-N used.
  * The CPU home and the B70 are measured elsewhere (out_cpu_worker_calib.txt, out_glm_cpu_calib.txt,
    out_ks_grid.txt); this file is only the 3090 leg.
