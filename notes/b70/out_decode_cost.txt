plan_decode_cost.py - the measured B70 decode cost model that replaces "bytes / GB-per-second".

Why this module exists. plan_operating_point.py and plan_concurrency.py computed B70 decode time as
`expert_bytes / rate(matched footprint)`, sampling a rate curve measured with memcpy-shaped launches. That
model was retired by measurement: the real GEMV was instruction-bound for every kernel up to rev H, and its
time is independent of the quantization recipe (Qwen3.8 across K=2..6: 73.6-74.8 us for identical weight
counts,
b70-kernels.md 2b/2c). Families, all bit-exact against a Python reference:

  dp4a             probe_gemv.cpp rev C with -DGEMV_KCONST  (compile-time trellis K; niche: unpermutable AND K>5)
  dpas (rev G)     probe_dpas4.cpp with DPAS_X=1  (tokens on M, experts in the grid) - superseded
  dpas_reg / _gather (revs H, I)                 register and gather operand routing - superseded
  dpas_transposed  rev J (probe_dpas7.cpp) / rev L (probe_dpas9.cpp): transposed tiles, the current kernel and
                   the one the close-out recommends the engine implement; needs the permuted device layout (D3)
  fused (E25a/b)   gate+up in ONE gemm launch at the KS that fills the grid, plus ONE combine_both launch

The engine dispatches by row count AND by grid occupancy, which is why the tables are indexed by rows and by E.

and the engine dispatches between them by row count, which is why the tables below are indexed by rows.

Every number is microseconds per (expert, projection) on Qwen3.8@2.68bpw, E=10, measured as the reported
projection time divided by E, with >= 1 s of CONTINUOUS executed work in the timing process (WARM_S; LESSONS 53)
and min-of-N interleaved with an anchor that warms itself (LESSONS 54 - >= 2000 reps, or the device is still at
its 1200 MHz idle clock and every number is ~2.2x off). Two windows were kept where they disagree, because they
sometimes disagree by 20 %. Cost is per weight, not per byte: these are the same weights at any trellis K, and
the quantization ladder measured that directly (3x bytes for +3% time at a small resident set,
out_quant_ladder.txt).

Provenance: notes/b70/out_gemv.txt (rev C / KCONST), notes/b70/out_dpas.txt (revs F/G/H and rev I =
probe_dpas6.cpp), out_revl.txt + out_dpas.txt (rev J = probe_dpas7.cpp and rev L = probe_dpas9.cpp, the current
fastest and the kernel the close-out recommends the engine implement), out_e25_fuse.txt (E25a gate+up fusion),
out_e25b_combine.txt (the single combine_both launch it enabled), out_ks_grid.txt (the per-KS joint choice), and
out_egrid.txt / out_low_e_grid.txt (E = 2..96). Dispatch is by recipe as well as rows:
the GATHER-family DPAS revisions (G/H/I) require K <= 5, so for those denser recipes fall back to dp4a; the
transposed family (rev J/L) does not - it serves K = 2..6 verified, 2..16 compiled.

cost spread note: two warm windows differ by up to ~20 % (rev G+X, down_proj, rows=1: 41.5 vs 50.9 us at E=10)

One kernel wins at every measured batch now: rev J (transposed tiles) beats rev I by 1.27-1.46x and
dp4a by ~8x at rows=16. Dispatch is a single family, with one caveat that is a design decision rather
than a tuning knob: rev J needs the device copy of the weights intra-tile permuted (dword q*K+i ->
i*8+q). Same bytes, same DRAM traffic, same output bits, ~1.5 ms one-time device permute for a 512 MB
resident set - but the device tensor stops being byte-identical to the checkpoint, which is the
property the 'native layout, no repack' constraint was protecting. If that constraint is kept
literally the fallback is dpas_gather (rev I) at +27-46%, still one kernel; dp4a is the only option
where no permutation is possible at all. Revs G/H carry no advantage now and remain for provenance.
Rows=2 is interpolated; nothing here extrapolates past the measured set 1/2/4/8/16.

E25a (last column): gate+up in ONE launch at KS=1 plus down separate. Faster than three separate
projections at every measured rows (x1.06 at rows=1, x1.17 at rows=16) and it halves the gemm launch
count - but it is CONDITIONAL (D8): it wins because the separate launches under-fill ~2048 hardware
thread slots. Use should_fuse(tn, KS, E); once one launch fills the machine, fusion costs ~7 %.

Binding constraint now: rev J runs the gate projection at 437 GB/s effective (5.86 MiB in 14.0 us)
against measured ceilings of 484 GB/s (stream slope) and 674 GB/s (copy peak) - ~90% of the
achievable streaming rate, so decode is bandwidth-bound in the sense the earliest design notes
assumed, which no kernel in this line achieved until now. Its own diagnostics say balanced rather
than dominated: NO_MAD (12.5) sits above both FETCH_ONLY (8.9) and ALU_ONLY (10.9), and the
components sum to 21.3 against a full 14.0 (~35% overlap). The remaining levers are bytes and
locality - placement, ordering, the 18.35 GiB threshold - not instructions.

rows > 16 - measured, not clamped (out_rows_gt16.txt). Above the kernel's 16-row coverage the
engine buckets, and bucketing is ADDITIVE: x2.00 / x2.98 / x3.96 at 32 / 48 / 64 rows, on both
projections - no L2 sharing between buckets of the same expert, so each re-pays the stream.
  rows=32   ( 2 buckets)  bucketed schedule    14.18 us/expert-layer   shared-stream kernel ~   9.83 us   (worth x1.44)
  rows=64   ( 4 buckets)  bucketed schedule    28.36 us/expert-layer   shared-stream kernel ~  15.30 us   (worth x1.85)
  rows=128  ( 8 buckets)  bucketed schedule    56.72 us/expert-layer   shared-stream kernel ~  26.24 us   (worth x2.16)
  rows=256  (16 buckets)  bucketed schedule   113.44 us/expert-layer   shared-stream kernel ~  48.13 us   (worth x2.36)
The bucketed column is what the engine does today. The shared-stream column was a PROJECTION that
E29 then BUILT AND TESTED, and it loses in every shape - gate rows=32: 41.5 us for two separate
16-row launches, 53.7 us sharing 2x16, 60.5 us sharing 4x8, and 64.8 us for a NATIVE single 32-row
launch (NF=4, also implemented and bit-exact). Reason: this kernel's 128-GRF budget holds exactly 16
rows spill-free, and every wider shape spills 1856-3584 B/thread to scratch, which costs more than
re-reading weights at the ~450 GB/s the kernel already achieves. So do NOT widen the row dimension;
fuse columns/projections instead (E25). And never pad a bucket: 24 rows as one padded NF=4 launch
costs 61.8 us, while 16+8 as two buckets costs ~38 us (b70-decode-closeout.md, E29 RESULT).
