Prefill at realistic chunk sizes (1024 / 2048 / 4096 tokens), all four mechanisms, against the shipped kernel
=============================================================================================================
The question this answers: at the chunk sizes a real prefill pass actually uses, what does the B70's MoE expert
leg cost, and which of the mechanisms tried today is fastest? Everything below is MEASURED on the card in one
window (WARM_S=3 continuous executed work, min-of-N, anchor restored to **19.1 us** — `DPAS_X=1 ./probe_dpas6
/tmp/gemv_dpE10 6000 1 2`, in the 19.0-19.4 protocol window; the anchor case dir had been lost with /tmp and was
regenerated, so for the first time today the whole series is anchored).

SHAPE. Qwen3.8@2.05 (K=2, integer K as the ring requires), E=512, gate/up k=4096 n=640 tn=40, down_proj
measured separately (not scaled), 48 MoE layers, topk 10, layer 12 geometry. Weights per projection at E=512 =
**251 MiB = 10.5x the 24 MiB L2** — the first series in this chapter where the working set genuinely exceeds
the cache, which is the regime the earlier E<=32 results could not speak to.

## 1. The four mechanisms at one point in shape space (E=512, KS=1, us per launch of ALL experts)

                rows/expert=16        32              64          scaling 16->64
  rev L (gate)      791.6              —               —         (rows<=16 cap; this is the shipped kernel)
  bucket A          788.4           1580.6          3198.4       4.06x  (exactly additive)
  slab co-sched B   827.0           1444.6          2701.8       3.43x  (sub-additive)
  rows-deep xmx     810.9           2457.3          8538.9      10.53x  (disqualified)
  down, bucket A   1335.2              —           5362.9        4.02x
  down, slab B     1333.4              —           4883.3        3.66x

FINDINGS, each with its control:
1. **Variant A IS the shipped kernel.** probe_gemm's bucket path measures 788.4 us against rev L's own 791.6 us
   on identical work — 0.4 % apart. So the A/B comparison below is a comparison against the fastest DPAS kernel
   in the tree, not against a weakened stand-in. (Down: A 1335.2 vs B 1333.4 at 1 slab, i.e. the two paths agree
   to 0.1 % when there is nothing to schedule, which is the sanity check that the 5 % gate gap at rows=16 was
   noise.)
2. **Co-scheduled slabs beat sequential buckets once the weights exceed L2, by 9-16 %.** B/A = 1.05 / 0.91 /
   0.85 at 1 / 2 / 4 slabs on gate, and 1.00 / — / 0.91 on down. This is the SIGN FLIP from the E=16 series
   (where B lost 15 % at 2 slabs): at E=16 both arms were near the 2048-slot capacity so B paid for crossing it,
   while at E=512 both arms are deep in many-wave territory and the only surviving difference is that A fetches
   the same 251 MiB four times in sequence and B fetches it four times concurrently. **The traffic law is
   therefore real and softening it is worth ~8-15 %, but only at scale** — measured at E=64 too (B/A = 0.82 at
   rows=64, 25 MiB = 1.05x L2, the crossover region).
3. **Rows-deep stays disqualified at every scale tested, and gets worse with scale**: at E=512 it is **2.67x
   worse** than bucket A at rows=64 (8538.9 vs 3198.4 us), having been 2.01x worse at E=16, and it scales 10.53x
   for 4x the rows. Note it does *tie* the shipped kernel where the register law is not yet binding — rows=16:
   810.9 vs rev L's 791.6 us, 2.4 % apart — which is what makes the divergence at rows>=32 a register story and
   not a general quality defect. Mechanism from the ISA is unchanged (`.spill size` 3392 B streamed at NF=8) but
   its cost *multiplies with the tile loop*, so it worsens as E grows: the spill is per-item-per-tile, and E=512
   runs 8x the tiles of E=16. An E=16-only measurement understated the penalty by ~4x — the reason this series
   exists.
4. **Fusion is not a lever at prefill scale** (as the fill rule predicted, now measured at E=512): rev L fused
   gate+up = 1621.7 us vs 2 separate launches = 1583.2 us, i.e. fused is 2.4 % WORSE. At decode's small E the
   fused schedule won because it filled idle slots; with 20480 items per launch there are no idle slots, so only
   its register cost remains. `best_launch` has no measured rung past E=96, so this point extends the fusion
   table's valid range downward in benefit, not upward.

## 2. Integrated over live routing: the expert leg per prefill chunk

Bucket counts come from the live per-layer profiles (`profiles/live/live_205.json`), cost per bucket from the
measurements above (gate/up 1.546 us, down 2.608 us per expert-16-row-bucket at E=512). **Expert leg only** —
attention, dense MLP, hyper-connections, norms and the 3090->B70 activation transfer are NOT in these numbers.

     R      touched/layer   rows/exp mean   max    buckets/layer   ms/chunk (48L)   tok/s
  1024          208            51.1         513        770.8         210.9          4856
  2048          208           102.2        1026       1396.5         382.1          5360
  4096          208           204.4        2052       2683.1         734.1          5580

Weight-traffic amplification implied by the bucket counts: **3.71x / 6.72x / 12.90x** over one read per touched
expert, at R = 1024 / 2048 / 4096. That matches b70-prefill.md §3's analytic 2.24 / 7.01 / 26.29 closely at
R=2048 and is lower at R=4096 because the live trace's support caps the tail (see caveats).

THE CROSS-CHECK THAT MAKES ME TRUST THE CHAIN: b70-prefill.md §3 predicted, purely from bytes, that reading
weights once would give 36.6k tok/s at R=2048 and that the 16-row bucket law would cost it ⌈rows/16⌉ times. The
measured amplification at R=2048 is 6.72x, and 36.6k / 6.72 = **5.45k tok/s** against the 5.36k measured here —
1.6 % apart. Byte model, probe measurement and routing integration agree.

SCALE-INVARIANCE WORTH KNOWING: buckets/layer ~= R*topk/16 + O(touched/2), i.e. dominated by total picks and
nearly independent of how the picks spread. At R=4096 that law says 2560 (measured 2683, the excess being
16-row rounding). So the expert leg's cost is LINEAR in R with slope (topk/16) x 5.70 us per token-layer
(= 3.7 us/token/layer measured), which means the tok/s figure is essentially flat above R~1024 — prefill on the
B70 does not get much better by batching harder, it gets better only if the amplification drops.

## 3. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

  * **Against today's stack**: measured parent prefill is 655 tok/s (2.05) / 479 tok/s (2.68) for the WHOLE
    step (`run_prefill_baseline.sh`). The B70 expert leg alone would run 2048 tokens in 382 ms -> 5360 tok/s of
    expert work. So the expert leg is not the obstacle and B70 prefill is worth ~8x — **but that comparison is
    asymmetric**: 655 tok/s includes attention/dense/transfer, and the non-expert part of a prefill step has
    never been measured (owed: `bench_engine_step.py` at prefill sizes). If the parent's non-expert prefill cost
    is ~1 ms/layer, end-to-end lands near 1.5-2k tok/s, not 5.4k. This is the single number needed to turn the
    table above into a plan statement.
  * Support caveat: "touched/layer = 208" is capped by the 192-token live trace, so at R=4096 real touched is
    closer to 450-512 and the max rows/expert (2052) understates the hot tail. Effect on the table is small
    (bucket totals are pick-dominated) and on the amplification figure it is favourable to the current numbers.
  * Not measured: rows/expert beyond 64 per single launch (probes cap at 64 rows, engine buckets beyond),
    KS at large E (20480 items make split-K pointless for fill; assumed, not shown), attention/dense/transfer,
    GLM at any of this (its 2.53 GiB/layer cannot be resident, so prefill there is a residency question first),
    and anything at bf16/fp8 accumulation.
  * The upside that got away, quantified: if registers allowed one read per chunk, R=2048 would be ~36.6k tok/s
    instead of 5.4k. Six times the prefill throughput is sitting behind the 128-GRF accumulator bound, and the
    two ways around it that this tree can see (rows-deep items; SLM-staged A) both measured worse. A third
    possibility, untested, is a narrower unpack pipeline that frees the ~25 GRF of temporaries so rows=32 fits
    spill-free — that is an ALU-structure change, not a scheduling one, and it is the only remaining route to
    the 6x.
