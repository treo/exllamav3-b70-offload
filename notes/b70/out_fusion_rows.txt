Does the fusion penalty band depend on rows? Edges no, depth yes - the penalty fills in as rows grow
=========================================================================================================
D8's second condition (a fused launch holds BOTH projections' weight streams live, so it stops paying once they
overflow L2) was established at rows=1 only. The rows-dependence was an explicitly untested prediction sitting in
`out_ks_grid.txt`, and `best_launch()` refused rows=8/16 for every E except 10/32/96 because of it. This file
closes it: 6 E x 3 rows x 2 schedules x 4 KS = 72 cells, one instrument, one window.

Instrument: `python3 notes/b70/sweep_ks_grid.py --bin ./probe_dpas9 --es 10,16,20,24,32,64 --rows 1,8,16
            --ks 1,2,4,8 --dirs-template '/tmp/ks_e{E}_gate:/tmp/ks_e{E}_up' --warm 2`
Window certification: the anchor printed 19.0-19.4 us in **all 72 cells** (min/median/max 19.0/19.2/19.4), and
every fused cell reported exact=yes (bit-exact against its own reference). Costs are per (expert, projection) for
the separate schedule and per (expert, gate+up pair) for the fused one, so they compare directly as 2x sep vs 1x
fused. Raw output is appended below this section.

WHAT THE ROWS-DIMENSION DOES AND DOES NOT DO
  * The band's EDGES do not move. E=20/24/32 preferred SEPARATE at rows=1 and still prefer separate at rows=16;
    E=10 preferred fused at every row count; E=64 was within 2-5% either way at every row count (4.0% toward
    fusing at rows=8 - just outside the 3% tie band, so `best_launch` reports a win rather than INDIFFERENT).
    Across 18 (E, rows) pairs the preference never flipped decisively - E=16 crossed the 1.00 line (1.08 -> 0.98)
    but both sides of it are inside the ~3% tie band, so it is "advantage disappears", not "sign reverses".
  * The band's DEPTH fills in as rows grow. Worst penalty per row count: **0.76x at rows=1, 0.88x at rows=8,
    0.90x at rows=16**. E=32 is the clean case: fusing cost 24% at rows=1 and 5% at rows=16.
  * Mechanism (directional, not proven): the penalty is a memory-side cost - two weight streams competing for a
    24 MiB L2 - while rows add arithmetic per weight byte. Same absolute thrash, bigger denominator, so the
    relative penalty shrinks. That is the same axis the bit-rate sweep found from the other side
    (`out_fusion_penalty_k.txt`: penalty deepens with bits, 1.16/1.27/1.88x at K=2/3/6, i.e. with bytes to thrash).
    Penalty ~ memory cost / (work per byte), shallow in neither variable alone.
  * Split-K still tracks fill, and rows supply it: the fused best KS falls 4 -> 2 -> 1 as rows grow at E=16/20,
    because the rows themselves now provide the parallelism split-K was buying. D8's joint-choice framing holds in
    the third dimension without amendment.

RULES THAT COME OUT OF THIS (applied)
  1. **One fusion decision per (model, quant), taken at rows=1.** Since the edges do not move and rows=1 is where
     the penalty is deepest, evaluating the cache warning at rows=1 is conservative for every larger batch. No
     per-rows dispatch of the fusion flag is needed, and none was added.
  2. The cache warning is only load-bearing at small rows. At rows>=8 everything measured is within 10% of the
     best schedule regardless of the choice, so mis-classifying a band-edge model costs little.
  3. Table coverage: `KS_SEP`/`KS_FUSED` gained rows=8/16 for E=16/20/24/64 and KS=4/8 for E=10/16/20/24/32/64 at
     those rows, so `best_launch()` no longer refuses rows=8/16 outside E=10/32/96. Existing cells were NOT
     overwritten — **46 cells overlap the earlier run (the rows=1 ladder was re-measured too): median deviation
     0.34%, p90 1.33%, max 3.17%, and 11 cells identical to the digit.** The 3.17% outlier is worth noticing
     because it is not in the noise of an unimportant cell — it is fused E=16 rows=1 KS=2, which is that
     schedule's *minimum* (2.175 in-tree, 2.244 here). So run-to-run scatter at the chosen optimum reaches ~3%,
     which is exactly the size of the 1.08x "fusion wins" at E=16 rows=1. Read that cell as a tie, and treat any
     `best_launch` margin under ~3% as unresolved between runs (the `~tie` marking already does this).

STILL UNMEASURED
  * rows=2 and rows=4 - the two row counts the older per-projection tables interpolate freely and the joint tables
    still refuse. Filling them would let `plan_operating_point.py` stop falling back to the legacy model in half
    its grid. Measured consequence: with rows=8/16 added, **no planner number moved at all** - the legacy
    per-projection tables were already KS-optimal, so the joint tables' value is that they state the shape and the
    tie explicitly, not that they price it differently.
  * The rows-dependence at K != 3 (this sweep is K=3 throughout), so the depth-vs-rows and depth-vs-bits
    interactions are each measured but never jointly.
  * E values between the ladder points (16/20/24 are 2-4 apart; fractional ownership is still an open item).
