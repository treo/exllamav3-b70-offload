WHAT SURVIVES THE SWEEP
1. Ordering beats layout, everywhere. clustered/native is 1.08-1.99x and never below 1.08;
   banded/native lives in 0.96-1.05 with no consistent sign. So "repack the tiles" is still not the
   lever, but the earlier claim that it is worth *exactly* nothing was an artifact of one footprint:
   on GLM's geometry it reaches +5 % at two sizes. Native order remains the decision because the gain
   is inconsistent, smaller than the ordering effect, and would be paid for with a repack pass at load
   and the loss of byte-interchangeability with the 3090's kernels (which expert migration needs).
2. The absolute scattered rate is NOT a card constant: 132-292 GB/s (Qwen3.8) and 132-260 (GLM)
   across a 64 MiB-2 GiB footprint, non-monotonic, with no monotone relation to grid size (Qwen3.8 at
   128 MiB has 104,800 items and reads at 200 GB/s; at 64 MiB it has half the items and reads at 292).
   The prime suspects are DRAM bank/hash aliasing against the address range and L2 reuse across the 4
   reps, neither of which this probe separates. Consequence for planning: a decode estimate built on
   one microbenchmark number carries up to ~2x uncertainty, so the planning figure must come from the
   real GEMV at real per-job footprints (E4), and the sweep's low end, not its middle, is what
   oversized/flat-routing models like GLM should be sized against.
3. Dense arithmetic stays within a few percent of pure reads at every footprint (R6 vs R2: -0.4 % to
   -14 %; the worst cases are the two smallest Qwen3.8 sets), so "decode is bandwidth-bound" holds,
   but its margin is footprint-dependent too. At 64 MiB Qwen3.8 the density penalty is 14 %, not 4 %.
