# Parent PREFILL decomposition by kernel — measured, engine-correct, one chunk at a time

Supersedes the first version of this file, which was a transcription of logs lost when the box hard-hung
(`/tmp` was tmpfs). Every number here comes from a log on disk: `notes/b70/logs/pr_*.log`, produced by
`notes/b70/bench_parent_prefill.py` via `run_plan_close2.sh` S1r.

Method recap (why this instrument and not another probe): the engine's OWN chunker splits the prompt, so cache
bookkeeping, transposition, attention regimes and MoE prefill paths are production ones — a job of
`(reps+1) × chunk` tokens with `max_new_tokens=1`, driven through `Job`/`gen.iterate()`. Device time is bucketed
by kernel name with `bench_engine_step.classify` imported, so the two benches cannot disagree about what counts
as `expert`. Corpus is `fixtures/prefill_pool.txt` (12 distinct docs — LESSONS 76), `-mct 15`, chunk 4096.

## The measurement

Device time per 4096-token chunk, milliseconds. "real work" = `other + gemm + norm + linstate + attn`, i.e.
everything the model does that is neither an expert GEMM nor a transfer.

| arm | tok/s | wall | device | busy | expert | **copy** | other | gemm | rest | **real work** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| q205 `-mcs 256`      | 1234 | 4129 | 2692 | 65 % | 769 | **756** | 580 | 461 | 125 | **1166** |
| q205 `-mcs 256 -mcpa`| 1366 | 3795 | 2747 | 72 % | 761 | **840** | 574 | 454 | 120 | **1148** |
| q205 `-mcs 448`      |  770 | 6408 | 5824 | 91 % | 911 | **3755** | 620 | 421 | 117 | **1158** |
| q268 `-mcs 384`      |  704 | 6764 | 5417 | 80 % | 842 | **3433** | 601 | 427 | 113 | **1141** |
| q405 `-mcs 448`      |  433 | 11158 | 11538 | **103 %** | 1294 | **8886** | 798 | 420 | 139 | **1852** |

Non-expert device time (the quantity the `-mcs` slope fit called the 1988 ms intercept): **1922.9 / 1986.4 /
4912.3 / 4574.7 / 10243.8 ms** for the five arms.

## Finding 1 — the intercept is NOT irreducible model work; it is mostly the tiering mechanism's own traffic

`-mcs 256` reproduces the wall-clock fit almost exactly (non-expert **1922.9 ms** vs the fitted intercept
**1988 ms**, 3.3 % apart, two completely different instruments: a linear fit over three `-mcs` values vs a
kernel-attribution sum). That agreement is why the number was trusted for a week. The decomposition says the
agreement was a coincidence of composition: of those 1922.9 ms, **756 ms (39 %) is `copy`** — `aten::copy_` plus
`Memcpy HtoD (Pinned→Device)` — and the model's actual non-expert compute is **1166 ms**.

And the copy term is not fixed: homing 192 more experts (`-mcs 256 → 448`) adds **2999 ms** of copy while adding
only 8 ms to `real work`. So "everything that does not scale with homed experts" (the intercept's definition)
contains a term that scales with homed experts *on the device side*. Consequences, in order of importance:

* The floor used in every prefill projection (D15's `1988 + 734 = 2722 ms`, "1.69×") charged mechanism overhead
  as if it were untouchable model work. The honest floor is **~1.15 s of real work per 4096-token chunk**, which
  is nearly quant-independent (1166 / 1148 / 1158 / 1141 across 2.05 and 2.68, 1852 at 4.05).
* The upside is therefore larger than 1.69× **if** a different home also lowers the transfer cost — and unchanged
  if it does not. That is now the pivotal question, not the expert GEMM rate: see the open item at the bottom.
* `-mcpa` is confirmed independent of this: same real work (1148 vs 1166), +84 ms of copy, and 1234 → 1366 tok/s.
  Its gain comes from moving expert work, exactly as D15 says, and it pays for it in copy.

## Finding 2 — the shipped CUDA path reproduces the bucket law, as *traffic*

`moe_unswizzle_kernel` calls per chunk track homing and quant the way `ceil(rows/cap)` predicts:

| arm | local experts | rows/local expert | unswizzle ms | calls/chunk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q205 `-mcs 256` | 256 | ~160 | 24.7 | 572 |
| q205 `-mcs 448` | 64 | ~640 | 125.9 | 2518 |
| q405 `-mcs 448` | 64 | ~640 | 188.4 | 5409 |

`EXL3_MOE_FUSED_ROWS` is 128 (clamped to [128, 512], `util/backend.py:49`), with the header comment "experts with
more assigned rows take the reconstruct paths". At `-mcs 448` there are 64 local experts carrying ~640 rows each
= 5 buckets apiece, and the call counts go up ~4.4–9.5× with a matching copy explosion (8886 ms at 4.05, 77 % of
device time). So the parent pays the same row-bucket arithmetic this plan measured on the card — but as **memory
traffic and reconstruction**, not as extra kernel time. Two independent implementations of the law, one in
production. This is the strongest support yet for D17 (keep buckets narrow) and D18 (cost per bucket, not per
byte), and it says the high-quant parent is transfer-bound on the offload path, not compute-bound.

## Finding 3 — device-busy can exceed 100 %, and that is information

q405 reports **103 %**: the sum of device time exceeds wall time because copy-engine activity overlaps compute.
Two readings, both useful: (a) "device-busy" is not a utilisation bound and must not be clipped to 100; (b) the
arms at 65–80 % (q205) have 20–35 % of the chunk wall unaccounted for by device work — that is the exposed CPU
worker wait, and it shrinks as more experts move to the CPU (`-mcs 256` 65 % → `-mcs 448` 91 %), i.e. at high
homing the parent stops stalling because it is instead drowning in copies.

This is the prefill counterpart of GLM decode's 71 % stall (`out_engine_step.txt` §11), and it bounds what the
`max(·)` model can claim: prefill has real overlap available, decode-shaped steps mostly do not.

## What this changes in the plan

1. **D15's arithmetic survives, its interpretation does not.** The substitution structure (promotion and the card
   remove the same term once) is intact, but the "floor" contains ~756 ms/chunk of offload transfer at the
   shipped configuration. Quote the floor as **real work ≈ 1.15 s + transfer ≈ 0.75 s**, not 1988 ms monolith.
2. **A new open item, ranked above further kernel work**: price the card's activation path — 3090→host-staged→B70
   versus a single copy if any P2P path exists across vendors — because the copy bucket is now the largest
   controllable term in prefill (28–77 % of device time), and if the card's home inherits the same host-staged
   round trip, much of the revised upside evaporates.
3. The first version of this file's "1.9× discrepancy" between non-expert and the intercept is resolved: it was
   the 33 % attribution bug in that script version (dividing buckets by `nchunks-1`), not a modelling insight.
