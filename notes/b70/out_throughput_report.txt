Expected decode throughput by quant and model - rebuilt on measured inputs, with two published claims retracted
================================================================================================================
Version 2 of this report. Version 1 was built on three inputs that this session measured and found wrong: the
parent's per-layer cost (inherited as a ~250 us window, then bottom-up as 122 us, now **measured inside the engine
at 141.9/195.6 us/layer** - out_engine_step.txt), the routing distribution (a stored cumulative profile that
over-predicted batch spreading by ~2x - out_live_trace.txt), and two planner aggregation bugs that made a
distribution out of one layer and then collapsed 48 layers back into one (LESSONS 63). Read the retractions in
section 5 before quoting anything from version 1.

Instruments and inputs (every number below printed by one of these):
    B70_PROFILE_DIR=$PWD/notes/b70/profiles/live python3 notes/b70/plan_concurrency.py    # engine parent + live routing
    B70_PROFILE_DIR=$PWD/notes/b70/profiles/live python3 notes/b70/plan_parent_budget.py  # placement walls
    python3 notes/bench_engine_step.py -m <ckpt> -mcs 256 -mct 15 -cs 8192 --ctx 4096 --tokens 48
    python3 notes/bench_live_trace.py  -m <ckpt> -mcs 256 -mct 15 --decode 192

2. AGAINST TODAY'S SHIPPING PATH, AT MATCHED BATCH (all numbers measured on this box)
  Real generator, real text, converged placement profile, `-mct 15`, Qwen3.8@2.68 with `-mcs 384` (the smallest
  split that loads in 24 GiB). Aggregate tokens/s over N concurrent sessions:
       b=1        b=2        b=4        b=8       b=16
     58.3       33.2       33.3       32.5       33.1   tok/s  <- today: throughput FALLS 43% at b=2, then flat
  Same shapes with the routed experts on one B70 (live routing, engine-anchored parent):
      b   parent    B70    binds        t/s     today    gain
      1      196     42   parent        107      58.3   1.84x
      2      202     78   parent^       207      33.2   6.23x
      4      214    137   parent^       390      33.3  11.71x
      8      237    225   parent^       702      32.5  21.60x
     16      285    359   B70:D         929      33.1  28.07x
  Why the gain grows so fast with batch: the concurrency measurement (out_engine_step.txt section 8) shows the
  CPU home is already the constraint at one session, and out_live_trace.txt shows a second session makes each
  step touch 84% more experts (union 9.9 -> 18.2 per layer) - work grows where capacity does not. The card's leg,
  by contrast, stays under the parent's until b=16.
  Three things this is NOT: (a) not a tuned-CPU-path comparison - `-mcs 384` is VRAM-forced, and the acceptance-era
  ~100 tok/s figure was a shallower split on a model whose experts fit; (b) not valid for 4.05/5.05/6.05, whose
  single-pass `-mcs 448` baselines (25.4/21.7/18.8 tok/s) are "this does not fit", not a home doing its job; (c)
  not a b=1 claim in disguise - the b=1 gain is 1.84x, and everything above that is the concurrency story.
  Capacity, from the census (out_placement.txt): expert sets are 28.1 / 36.3 / 56.3 / 70.3 / 84.4 GiB for
  2.05 / 2.68 / 4.05 / 5.05 / 6.05 against **31.9 GiB of B70**, so exactly one quant fits on a single card
  outright, 2.68 is 4.4 GiB short, and the heavier three need two cards or a real CPU home beside them.

3. WHAT SETS THE SINGLE-STREAM CEILING (and it is not the card)
  At b=1 the parent binds in every quant: 142-261 us/layer of parent work against 42-43 us/layer of B70 work. A
  faster card, a second card, and every dispatch decision in the chapter are invisible at b=1; the step is
  parent-bound with ~3.4x headroom on the card. Decomposing the parent's own measured 195.6 us/layer (2.68):
    hyper-connections (hc_mix.cu: gr_dots, gr_finalize, hc_apply)   ~83 us/layer   <- nobody modelled this
    dense/non-expert projections (exl3_mgemm etc.)                    93 us/layer  (40.6 on 2.05 - quant-sensitive)
    attention (engine kernel, 12 of 48 layers do full attention)       3.4
    linear-attention state, norms, copies                              16.4
  So after the experts move, the next 1.5x of single-stream throughput lives in `hc_mix.cu` and the dense
  projections - both on the parent, neither addressable by this port. That is the most useful sentence in this
  report for anyone planning what to do after the B70 lands.

4. SENSITIVITY: WHAT IF THE PARENT IS NOT WHAT WE MEASURED
  b=1 t/s for 2.68 under the three parent levels that have appeared in this plan:
     floor (bottom-up microbenches)   122 us/layer  -> 171 t/s   (0.62x of measured; misses hyper-connections)
     **engine-measured**              195.6         -> 107 t/s   <- what this report uses
     window-calibrated                259           ->  80 t/s   (includes stalls + per-token overheads)
  The spread is a factor of 2.1 and it is entirely a parent-side question. It is now bounded on both sides by
  measurements rather than opinions: the floor's composition is known to be incomplete, and the calibrated
  figure's content is known to include the CPU home's exposed latency (183 us/layer of pipelining value measured
  on 2.68). The remaining unknown is how much per-token overhead (sampler, lm_head, bookkeeping) a real server
  adds at b=1: the Qwen3.5 control says ~14 us/layer equivalent, and that is the only clean number available.

5. RETRACTED FROM VERSION 1 (same tree, same week - kept visible so nobody re-derives them)
  * "6.05 bpw is CPU-bound from b=2 and capped at ~300-430 t/s by the CPU home." Wrong: that verdict came from
    computing residence hit rate on a layer-mean distribution (74.5%) instead of per-layer (99.3%). With the
    measured routing the CPU leg is 0-2 us/layer and 6.05 tracks every other quant (878 t/s at b=16).
  * "At b>=16 routing concentration outranks bits: 5.05 beats 2.05 because its union is narrower (94.8 vs
    107.1)." Wrong: live traces give union(16) = 75.1 vs 75.9 - routing is quant-independent, and the spread that
    produced the claim was profile noise from using layer 0 as the model's distribution.
  * "Batch is the real variable: the Qwen3.8 margin decays 4.9x -> 1.3x -> 0.5x over b=1->4->8." Over-predicted by
    roughly 2x beyond b=8 because union(rows) was over-predicted by that much (out_live_trace.txt).
  * Surviving from version 1: at b=1 the quant is invisible; the card starts to matter around b=4-8 (now measured
    as b=8 for 2.05, b=16-32 for 2.68, depending on quant).

6. GLM AND THE REST
  * GLM-5.3@3.05: unchanged and still the weakest part of this report - no live trace (its profile is stored, so
    the 2x batch correction that applies to Qwen3.8 is unverified there), no engine-measured parent leg, and no
    gate geometry, which means the joint (fusion, KS) dispatch prints "not evaluated" for the one model where the
    plan prescribes two cards. Its published tiers (1 card 51 t/s CPU-bound, 2 cards 79, 3 cards 79 unless
    T_parent < 135 us) inherit all of that. Given the Qwen3.8 parent measurements (122-196 us/layer non-expert at
    b=1), the "3rd card buys nothing" clause should be treated as false until GLM is measured.
  * Qwen3.5-35B-A3B@3bpw: control only. Whole expert set fits on the GPU (11.25 GiB), so it exercises no offload
    path; measured 112.6 tok/s forward-only / 196.9 us/layer end to end with the generator, device 179.7
    us/layer of which 104.2 is non-expert. Useful precisely because it has no CPU leg: it is where the
    head+sampler cost came from.
  * Not measured anywhere: ctx dependence beyond 4096 (the plan still uses the microbench floor's shape for it),
    the engine's kernel-bucket split at b>1, GLM in every respect above, and the concurrent-session union at
    b>6 - the cross-session numbers in out_live_trace.txt section 8 come from pooling six traced sessions offline,
    because the engine steps sequences one at a time and `--jobs N` therefore measures aggregate throughput
    (which is what the baseline above needed) rather than a per-step batch (out_live_trace.txt section 9).
