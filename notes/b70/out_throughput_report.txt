Expected decode throughput by quant and model - rebuilt on measured inputs, with the retracted claims listed
=============================================================================================================
Version 3. Everything below is printed by the commands in the last section, on measured inputs:
  * parent cost: **engine-measured** (out_engine_step.txt) - not the 122 us microbench floor, not the 259 us
    window-calibrated figure, both of which are now known to be wrong in explained ways;
  * routing: **live per-token traces** (out_live_trace.txt), aggregated per layer - the stored profile
    over-predicted batch spreading by ~2x by b=32;
  * dispatch: the joint (fusion, split-K) table at the row decode actually uses, which required rows 2/4 to be
    measured (out_rows_2_4.txt). Before that, the rule was silently inert in this planner's grid and every
    number here was ~15-35% too expensive (LESSONS 67).

Reproduce:
    cd notes/b70
    B70_PROFILE_DIR=$PWD/profiles/live python3 plan_concurrency.py       # this report's tables
    B70_PROFILE_DIR=$PWD/profiles/live python3 plan_operating_point.py   # per-layer curves, per-model dispatch

    python3 notes/bench_live_trace.py -m <ckpt> -mcs 384 -mct 15 --jobs N --decode 64   # the baselines

is beyond the measured b=1/ctx 4096 point, so the level is anchored and the shape borrowed from the calibrated
curve; `:J`/`:D` = which measured schedule the dispatch chose (joint / per-projection).

2. AGAINST TODAY'S SHIPPING PATH, AT MATCHED BATCH (all measured on this box)
  Qwen3.8@2.68, real generator, real text, converged placement, `-mct 15`, `-mcs 384` (smallest split that loads
  in 24 GiB). Aggregate tokens/s over N concurrent sessions:
       b=1        b=2        b=4        b=8       b=16
     58.3       33.2       33.3       32.5       33.1   tok/s  <- today: falls 43% at b=2, then flat
  Same shapes with the routed experts on one B70:
      b   parent    B70    binds        t/s     today    gain
      1      196     38   parent        107      58.3   1.84x
      2      202     62   parent^       207      33.2   6.23x
      4      214    110   parent^       390      33.3  11.71x
      8      237    202   parent^       702      32.5  21.60x
     16      285    337   B70:J         990      33.1  29.91x
  Why the gain grows with batch: today's CPU home is already saturated at one session, and a second session makes
  each step touch ~84% more experts (union 9.9 -> 18.2 per layer, measured), so work grows where capacity does
  not. The card's leg stays under the parent's until b=16.

  What this is NOT, stated plainly:
  (a) not a tuned-CPU-path comparison. `-mcs 384` is VRAM-forced; the acceptance-era ~100 tok/s figure was a
      shallower split on a model whose experts fit. The claim is "today's deployable configuration for a 36 GiB
      expert set does not scale with concurrency", not "a CPU home cannot".
  (b) not valid for 4.05/5.05/6.05, whose single-pass `-mcs 448` baselines (25.4/21.7/18.8 tok/s) are "this does
      not fit", not a home doing its job. For those the honest statement is capacity: expert sets are 28.1 /
      36.3 / 56.3 / 70.3 / 84.4 GiB (2.05/2.68/4.05/5.05/6.05) against 31.9 GiB of B70 - one quant fits outright,
      2.68 is 4.4 GiB short, the rest need a second card or a real CPU home beside them.
  (c) not a b=1 claim in disguise: the b=1 gain is 1.84x, and everything above that is the concurrency story.

3. WHAT SETS THE SINGLE-STREAM CEILING (it is not the card)
  At b=1 the parent binds for every quant: 142-261 us/layer of parent work against 38-39 us/layer of B70 work -
  a ratio of 3.7x (2.05) to 6.7x (6.05), i.e. the card has that much headroom, so at b=1 a second card, a faster
  kernel and every dispatch decision in this chapter are invisible. Decomposing the parent's measured 195.6
  us/layer (2.68):
    hyper-connections (hc_mix.cu: gr_dots, gr_finalize, hc_apply)   ~83 us/layer   <- in no model in this plan
    dense/non-expert projections (exl3_mgemm etc.)                    93.0 us/layer (40.6 on 2.05)
    attention (engine kernel; 12 of 48 layers do full attention)       3.4
    linear-attention state, norms, copies                             16.4
  After the experts move, the next 1.5x of single-stream throughput lives in `hc_mix.cu` and the dense
  projections - both on the parent, neither reachable by offloading. That is the most useful sentence here for
  planning what comes after the B70 lands.

4. SENSITIVITY: IF THE PARENT LEVEL IS NOT WHAT WE MEASURED
  b=1 t/s for 2.68 under the three parent levels this plan has used:
     floor (bottom-up microbenches)   122 us/layer  -> 171 t/s   (0.62x of measured: no hyper-connection term)
     **engine-measured**              195.6         -> 107 t/s   <- used above
     window-calibrated                259           ->  80 t/s   (bundles the CPU home's exposed stall)
  The spread is 2.1x and it is entirely parent-side. Both bounds are now measurements of something: the floor's
  composition is known incomplete, and the calibrated figure's content is known to include 183 us/layer of
  unhidden delegation. The one genuinely unmeasured term is per-token overhead for Qwen3.8 (sampler + lm_head):
  the Qwen3.5 control, which has no CPU leg to hide, gives ~14 us/layer equivalent.

5. RETRACTIONS (each was published in this tree and is wrong)
  * "6.05 bpw is CPU-bound from b=2 and capped near 300-430 t/s." Artifact of computing residence hit rate on a
    layer-mean distribution (74.5%) instead of per layer (99.3%). CPU leg measured live: 0-2 us/layer.
  * "Routing concentration outranks bits at b>=16: 5.05 beats 2.05." Routing is quant-independent - union(16) =
    75.1 vs 75.9 - and all five quants land within 878-969 t/s at b=16, ordered by parent level, not by bits.
  * "Batch margin decays 4.9x -> 1.3x -> 0.5x over b=1->4->8." Union over-predicted ~2x by b=32; the decay is
    much gentler than the plan carried.
  * "With rows=8/16 added, no planner number moved." The joint path was not executing at all in that planner
    (rows=2 refused, silently caught). With rows 2/4 measured, GLM b=1 moved 129 -> 85 us/layer and Qwen3.8
    41 -> 34. LESSONS 67.

6. GLM AND THE OTHER PROFILES
  * GLM-5.3@3.05 now has a measured gate geometry (out_glm_geometry.txt: tiles_n=128, 3.0 MiB/expert/projection)
    and its own joint table, which says DO NOT FUSE at E>=6 (the separate launches already over-fill) and fuse
    only at E=4. That changed its per-layer costs by -34% at b=1 (129 -> 85 us/layer), so the tier arithmetic in
    architecture.md section 6 (1 card 51 t/s CPU-bound, 2 cards 79, 3 cards 79) is stale in the optimistic
    direction: cheaper B70 layers move the crossover where cards stop paying.
  * Still missing for GLM: an engine-measured parent leg, a live routing trace (its 19.14x multiplier at b=32 is
    inherited from a stored profile that we now know runs ~2x hot on Qwen), and any measurement at its own
    footprint scale on more than one card. Given the Qwen3.8 parent measurements, "the third card buys nothing"
    should be treated as false until GLM is measured.
  * Qwen3.5-35B-A3B@3bpw: control only (whole expert set fits on the GPU, 11.25 GiB). Measured 104.2 us/layer of
    non-expert device work, 179.7 total, 196.9 us/layer end to end. It is where the head/sampler figure comes
    from; it exercises no offload path, so quoting a B70 gain for it would be inventing one.
