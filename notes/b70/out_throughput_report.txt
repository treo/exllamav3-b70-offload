Expected decode throughput by quant and model, with both parent estimates side by side
=======================================================================================
What the planners say now that (a) the parent leg is measured (`out_parent_leg.txt`), (b) the CPU home is measured
per assignment (`out_cpu_worker_calib.txt`), and (c) the layer is priced from the joint (fusion, split-K) tables
(`out_ks_grid.txt`, `out_fusion_rows.txt`). Every number below was printed by a command named at the end; nothing
here is hand-composed except the two-column presentation.

WHY TWO t/s COLUMNS: the parent's non-expert cost per layer has two defensible values that differ ~2.1x - 257 us
from the planner model calibrated to the inherited ~250 us/layer window (contains sampler, lm_head, graphs, the
rest of the network) and 122 us measured bottom-up on the 3090 (attention + streamed non-expert weights + state
traffic only, explicitly a floor). LESSONS 61 records why averaging them would be wrong. Where a row shows the
same t/s in both columns, the B70 or the CPU - not the parent - is the binding leg, so the disagreement cannot
reach that row.

FIVE THINGS THIS TABLE SAYS
1. **At b=1 the quant does not matter, in either parent model.** All five quants land on 80-81 t/s (calibrated) or
   171 t/s (floor) because the B70's whole contribution is 36-42 us of a 122-259 us/layer step. Making the experts
   infinitely fast changes nothing at b=1 - it is not on the critical path. Anyone quoting "t/s per quant" at
   single-stream decode is quoting the parent, not the card.
2. **The card starts to matter at b=4-8**, exactly where its leg crosses the parent's: B70 167-174 us vs parent
   144 (floor) at b=4, 299-330 vs 176 at b=8. Below that crossover, extra B70 speed is invisible; above it, t/s is
   set by the card and the two parent columns collapse to identical values (b>=16 rows agree to the digit).
3. **6.05 bpw is CPU-bound from b=2 upward** - the home, not the card, is the limit (CPU leg 151-1565 us vs B70
   82-852). It is also the only quant where the placement decision shows up in throughput: as measured it caps
   6.05 at ~300-430 t/s while 2.05/5.05 reach 680-1060. Under the current residence policy this is a *policy*
   artifact (too many experts homed on the CPU for a 3.6 MiB/expert profile), not a hardware law - the fix is
   placement, and `plan_parent_budget.py`'s lpt columns are where that gets argued.
4. **Bits-per-weight is not what ranks the quants at b>=16; routing concentration is.** 5.05 beats 2.05 at b=16
   (761 vs 677) and b=32 (1058 vs 872) despite having 2.5x the bytes per expert, because its census shows a much
   narrower union (94.8 vs 107.1 distinct experts at b=16, 129.9 vs 158.5 at b=32) and 100% residence. The
   per-expert byte saving of the small quant is being spent on touching more experts. Confirming that on the
   engine (same router, different quant => same routing?) is a cheap check with a real consequence for quant
   selection at batch.
5. **The parent-model disagreement is a b<=2 phenomenon only.** At b=1 the two columns differ 2.1x (81 vs 171);
   by b=8 it is 5%; by b=16 it is zero. So planning batch sizes does not need the disagreement resolved - planning
   single-stream latency absolutely does, and it cannot be resolved on this box without instrumenting the engine's
   own step (sampler, lm_head, graphs).

GLM-5.3-Flash @3.05 (42 MoE layers, topk=8, 288 experts, 9.0 MiB/expert, one B70 owning h=0.708)
  * B70 leg from `plan_operating_point.py`: **129 us/layer at b=1** (50.8 MiB/layer footprint, outside L2's reach,
    132 GB/s effective), 5.43 ms/token of B70 time at b=1, 65.4 ms/token at b=16.
  * End-to-end tiers in `doc/port/b70-architecture.md` section 6: 1 card **51 t/s** (CPU-bound, 467 us/layer),
    2 cards **79 t/s** (+55%), 3 cards **79 t/s** (+0%), assuming `T_parent` = 300 us/layer.
  * Two of those caveats are now testable rather than assumed, and one flips: the Qwen parent measurements put the
    non-expert parent cost at 122-257 us/layer at b=1, not 300, and the 3-card break-even was stated as "the third
    card buys nothing unless T_parent < 135 us". **Under the measured floor (122 us) that inequality is satisfied**,
    so the third card's zero is an artifact of the assumed 300 us, not of the hardware. GLM's own parent leg is
    still unmeasured (different shapes, bigger hidden size), so the honest statement is: 1 card 51 t/s CPU-bound is
    solid, 2 cards ~79 is solid, and "3 cards add nothing" should be treated as false until GLM's parent leg is
    measured - the analogous measured parent would make the third card pay.
  * Its B70 column also still divides bytes by N at the same rate and drives per-card footprint to 32 MiB/layer,
    below the smallest GLM geometry measured - both unchanged from when that table was written.

Qwen3.5-35B-A3B @3bpw (40 layers, 256 experts, 1.12 MiB/expert, 11.25 GiB of experts total)
  * Not modelled end-to-end anywhere in this tree. What is known: the entire expert set fits on one B70 (11.25 GiB
    of 31.9), so there is no capacity pressure and no CPU home to fight over, and it is the one profile in
    `plan_placement.py` with h=100% and zero CPU spill. Expected behaviour by analogy with Qwen3.8's b=1 row:
    parent-bound single-stream, card invisible. Quoting a t/s for it would be inventing a number.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THESE NUMBERS REAL
  * The engine's own measured step time at b=1 on this host (closes the 122-vs-257 question and turns every
    "t/s-cal" row into a measurement rather than a calibration).
  * A real-checkpoint expert-bytes run of the parent leg (shapes/kernel real, bytes synthetic at K=3).
  * GLM's parent leg, for the reason in its section above.
  * Placement re-optimisation for 6.05, which is currently capped by a policy choice.
