Verify-batch economics (M = 4-16): the win is deduplication across the batch, not a bigger M tile
===================================================================================================
Where the near-term payoff actually is. Speculative decode asks the MoE layer to process M rows at once (MTP
verify, DFlash draft-verify, MTP+draft trees) with M in the 4-16 range, and the temptation - mine included, in
`doc/port/b70-prefill.md` - is to reach for a GEMM. The measurements in this tree say the gain at those shapes
comes from a different place, and they bound how far it goes.

WHAT IS MEASURED vs COMPUTED. Kernel costs are the measured rev L joint-table values (gate+up pair + down per
expert, best (fused?, KS) per (E, rows); `plan_decode_cost.best_launch`), which are flat in rows precisely
because the tokens-on-M layout (kernels.md M15/M16) makes the marginal row ~5% of an expert read. Routing is
the live per-token expert sets from `notes/b70/profiles/live/live_268.json` (48 layers x 192 tokens), so
"which experts does a batch touch" is measured, not modelled. What is *computed* is the combination of the two
and the ceilings; nothing here is measured end-to-end on a real verify step.

BATCHED COST (A: picks bucketed by expert, each expert costs rows/expert) vs M INDEPENDENT DECODES (B):

(dedup headroom = A / cost of the same M rows if every row hit the same 10 experts, i.e. zero spread.)

THREE THINGS THE TABLE SAYS
1. **Batching M=4-16 pays 1.34-1.85x on the card, and it is worth ~2.5x at M=32.** Per-token layer cost falls
   35.8 -> 19.4 us from M=1 to M=16. That is a real, near-term number that needs no new kernel.
2. **Almost all of it is deduplication, not row-scaling.** At M=16 the 160 picks land on 73 experts instead of
   160 (a 2.2x reduction in weight reads), while rows/expert is only 2.19 - which is why the measured win
   (1.85x) sits well below the 16x that "one read, sixteen rows" would suggest. The M tile is *starved by
   routing spread*, so a wider DPAS tile buys almost nothing at these shapes: kernels.md 2c item 2's own rule
   (tile height <= rows/expert, `MS_=1`/16-row because a 64-row tile "wastes ~60/64 rows") already puts the
   right tile in place at rows/expert ~2. The near-term work is the bucketing and the gather, not the tile.
3. **The ceiling is 4.9x at M=16 (10.2x at M=64), and reaching it is a scheduling problem.** The headroom
   column is what batching would save if the batch's rows shared experts perfectly. Two ways to move toward
   it, both cheap to try and both about co-scheduling rather than arithmetic:
     * pack verify requests whose drafts share hot experts onto the same worker/B70 (the live profile says
       half of a layer's picks fall in 23 of 512 experts, so overlap between concurrent sessions is large and
       findable);
     * and the `probe_slm` result (out_prefill_gemm.txt) makes the multi-row case cheaper than it looks: when
       several row-slabs of one expert are *concurrent* (same work-group), 31 of 32 weight reads are absorbed
       by the hierarchy at zero DRAM cost - so the marginal row really is ~5% of a read, and rows/expert does
       not have to be large for reuse to pay.

WHAT THE ENGINE NEEDS TO COLLECT IT
  * bucket-by-expert over the verify batch with the activation gather folded into the load (kernels.md 2c
    items 5 and 11: `chunks = {eid, t0, nt}` + `tok_ids`, so there is no `index_select` round trip). At M=16
    this is a 73-entry bucket list per layer, not a new data structure.
  * the decode job slots sized for M rows: the CPU path's `MAX_M = 8` and the B70 budget's `slots x slot_rows`
    both cap batch rows today; M=16 verify needs the slot math redone, and `EXL3_MOE_B70_DPAS_T` (tentatively
    16) is the knob that decides GEMV-vs-tile at these shapes.
  * the combine over topk partials per row (unchanged from decode; M rows just multiplies it, and it is
    already the cheap stage - `combine_both` at 2.9-3.8 us/launch).
  * correctness surface: a verify batch must produce bit-identical per-row results to M separate decodes at
    the same inputs, because a drafter's accept/reject decision depends on exact logits. That is Gate B3 with
    an M dimension, and it is the thing that will actually break first (partial-sum ordering per row).
  * the MTP/DFlash path currently runs experts on the parent (`exllamav3/model/spec_decode_mtp.py`,
    `spec_decode_drafters.py`): nothing in the verifier consults the offload homes. So the first integration
    step is plumbing, and the payoff bound is the 1.34-1.85x measured above at the card level - which, at
    decode's b=1 constraint (parent binds at 196 us/layer vs the card's 38), means the *end-to-end* verify win
    is capped by how much of the verify step is expert work. Verify at M=16 changes that balance: the card's
    310.6 us/layer for the batched batch is above the parent's non-expert 195.6, so at M>=16 the card becomes
    the binding leg again and the batching decision starts to show up in tokens/s. That is the sharpest reason
    to do this before big-M prefill: M=16 is where the card stops being free.

CAVEATS
  * Cost tables are measured for decode-shaped launches at E up to 96; a verify batch touches ~73-139 experts,
    near the top of that range, and the tables are ~10-20% pessimistic for large resident sets, so the real
    batched numbers should be slightly better than A above.
  * rows/expert > 16 in the M=32/64 rows is priced as buckets (additive). `probe_slm` says that additive law is
    an artifact of time-separated launches, so a co-scheduled kernel should bend it - that is the single most
    valuable thing `probe_gemm` can measure at these M, more valuable than any big-M tile.
  * The routing overlap between *concurrent* verify sessions is unmeasured (the traces are single-session);
    the "pack drafts that share hot experts" idea needs a two-session trace before it is more than a hunch.
  * Nothing here is measured on a real verify step; the numbers are a bound on where the win is, and the first
    experiment should be the plumbing plus the M=8/M=16 batch on real MTP accept rates.
