WHAT THE MATCHED COMPARISON SAYS
1. **Concurrent re-reads of the same bytes are not a cost — they are cheaper than a unique stream.** Reuse at
   40 KiB/32 delivers 1690 GB/s of unique bytes while OWNED, which never repeats a byte, manages 1349. Thirty
   -one copies of every read are absorbed on-chip (100% L1 hit, 54 TB/s of request-side bandwidth) and the
   DRAM side still moves each byte once. Directionally consistent with llm-scaler's group-major rule (neigh-
   bouring groups should touch the same bytes), but see the CONFIDENCE block in out_prefill_gemm.txt: this
   probe's absolute rates are contaminated by address arithmetic, so this is a hint to test in `probe_gemm`,
   not a design number.
2. **SLM staging is 1.5-6x slower than the same walk from global, and it scales badly.** At the tile size a
   real kernel wants (80 KiB, one expert-projection) it is 6.0x worse; at 20 KiB only 1.5x. The ISA gives the
   mechanism without speculation: the staged body is 2382 instruction lines vs 1749/1754 for the two global
   modes, carrying all 80 of the global `load.ugm.d32x4` (the staging read is unavoidable) *plus* 80
   `load.slm.d32x4` and 89 `store.slm.*`. Staging therefore adds a round trip through local memory rather
   than replacing traffic — and the SLM pipe is far narrower than the global path.

1. WHY THE FIRST VERSION OF THIS FILE WAS WRONG
The initial sweep compared a vectorized global walk against a scalar SLM walk: the global modes emitted
`load.ugm.d32x4.a64` while the staged path emitted scalar `load.slm.d32`/`store.slm.d32`, because the staged
loop's per-lane addresses were strided by 16 dwords and the compiler would not vectorize that. Any
staged-vs-global conclusion from that table was an access-width artifact in disguise. Fixed by making the
staged walk lane-contiguous (`local_addr[(li % 4) + j*4 + i*128]`), which is both what a real kernel would do
and what vectorizes. Verified in the ISA rather than assumed — all three modes now carry the same
`load.ugm.d32x4` count (80), and the SLM mode's extra work is exactly the local round trip:

    notes/b70/asm_slm/mode0_OCL_..._entry_0001.asm   ugmd32x4=80  slm_ld=0   slm_st=0   (OWNED)
    notes/b70/asm_slm/mode1_OCL_..._entry_0008.asm   ugmd32x4=80  slm_ld=0   slm_st=0   (REUSE)
    notes/b70/asm_slm/mode2_OCL_..._entry_0015.asm   ugmd32x4=80  slm_ld=80  slm_st=89  (SLM)

LESSON (the general one, filed as LESSONS 68): when comparing two memory *mechanisms*, verify in the ISA that
the compiler gave both the same access *width and shape*. Here the artifact of the first run would have
"proven" SLM was 2.5x slower for a reason that had nothing to do with SLM. A mismatch that large is invisible
in the timing output and obvious in one grep.

2. WHAT SURVIVES, AND WHAT THE PREFILL KERNEL THEREFORE DOES
  * Survives: SLM staging is the wrong mechanism (1.5-6x, mechanism identified). The design in
    `doc/port/b70-prefill.md` §4a accordingly maps (expert, column tile, k-slice) to a work-group and each
    16-row slab to a work-item, reads B globally and concurrently, and stages nothing. That part of the design
    does not depend on the contested re-use rate — it is the cheaper of the two mechanisms measured.
  * Open, and now the central question: whether a real kernel can beat the additive `ceil(rows/16)` traffic
    law (E29, measured on sequential buckets). `probe_gemm` variants A (today's bucket ordering) vs B
    (concurrent slab ordering) decide it, scored on bytes moved per chunk; both get ISA dumps, and the dumps
    must show matching load widths before their timings are compared — which is the mistake this whole
    detour exists to prevent repeating.
  * Not established: any absolute prefill GB/s from this probe.
