# E24 step 1 - integer XMX DPAS with byte-replicated operands: does the matrix path remove the
# per-row instruction slot?  (probe_dpas.cpp, notes/b70; measured 2026-10-29)

## What is being claimed and by what method

rev C costs `slots/weight ~= 5.8 + 1.15 * rows` (fitted from its own measured 205.5 -> 136.4
G-weights/s for rows 1 -> 4 against the 1.43e12 slot/s issue budget). The DPAS formulation under
test (b70-kernels.md 2c item 13b) replaces the byte-SUM with the four BYTES of
`window * 0x83DCD12D` as four K-steps, paired with the activation replicated 4x along K:

    sum_kb A[col][kb] * B[kb][t]  =  sum_k bytesum(prod_k) * x_k      exact, in int32

so nothing needs more than 8 bits (A is u8 as produced, B is s8), the accumulator is int32, and the
epilogue `k_inv * q * (acc - 510 * sum_x8)` is unchanged. Shape M=8, K=32, N=16, sub-groups of 16,
all proven reachable on this card by doc/b70/b70-bench (366.92 TOPS = 100% of Intel's 367).

STEP 1 DELIBERATELY MAKES OPERAND ASSEMBLY FREE. A comes from abytes.bin written by the same
authoritative unpacker that writes y_ref (dump_gemv_case.py --with-abytes), NOT from a second C++
unpacker. That isolates "does the matrix math scale flat with rows?" from the expensive engineering
question of whether a real kernel can assemble A in registers without paying more than it saves
(llm-scaler's own warning: "assembling it with strided register moves cost more than the DPAS
itself saved"). The cost of this choice: A is 4 bytes per weight vs the trellis's K/8 = 0.375, i.e.
10.7x the bytes at K=3, so GB/s is NOT comparable with rev C and every DPAS number below carries a
byte-traffic handicap that a shipping kernel would not have.

## Correctness

    DPAS bit-exact vs y_ref: True   max|d| 0.0        (E=10, gate_proj, rows=1, KS=4)

Gate A survives untouched - no float anywhere in the path. Getting there caught one bug worth
recording: declaring the B operand `uint8_t` compiles, runs at full speed, and turns every negative
activation into 128..255, inflating the accumulator to ~1.6e8 (the all-same-sign bound) instead of
~5e5. dpas.hpp allows A and B to have different signedness precisely so this mix is expressible.
Note that b70-bench's all-ones self-check is structurally BLIND to this class of bug.

## Rows scaling at the b=1 operating expert count (E=10, Qwen3.8 layer 0, k=2560, n=640)

weights = 16.384 M; each cell is min of 3 interleaved runs, burst-of-8 throughput mode.
G-wt/s = weight-tokens per second = the metric that says "how many tokens' worth of work per second".

 rows |   revC us | revC G-wt/s |   revD us | revD G-wt/s | revD vs revC (same work)
-----:|----------:|------------:|----------:|------------:|---------------------------
    1 |      75.5 |         217 |     144.0 |         114 | x0.52
    2 |      78.7 |         417 |     145.1 |         226 | x0.54
    4 |     112.5 |         583 |     146.6 |         447 | x0.77
    8 |     251.7 |         521 |     153.5 |         854 | x1.64
   16 |     485.5 |         540 |     159.2 |        1648 | x3.05

rev C's weight-token throughput SATURATES at ~520-580 G-wt/s from rows 4 on - that is the issue-rate
ceiling, and it is the clearest possible statement that decode is instruction-bound. rev D's time grows
only 144 -> 159 us (+11%) across a 16x row increase, and its weight-token throughput keeps climbing to
1648 G-wt/s, which is still only 3.6% of the 45.9 T weight-token ceiling the matrix unit allows at
4 byte-MACs per weight-token - i.e. rev D at E=10 is bound by streaming A, not by math.

Here DPAS wins at EVERY row count, including rows=1, and its rows curve is FLAT to within 3% from
rows 4 to 16 (18.3 -> 18.8 us) while rev C's rises 51 -> 95 us. That flatness is the direct answer to
E24: the per-row marginal cost of the matrix path is ~0, exactly as predicted, because the row
dimension is paid for by the matrix unit's N dimension instead of by instructions.
rev C's rows=16 point (82.3 us, faster than its own rows=8) is measurement noise at E=1, where there
are few work-groups and the run-to-run spread is large (LESSONS 28) - the direction of the effect is

## What this decides

1. Integer DPAS with byte replication is bit-exact and its rows cost is ~0. The 1.15-slots-per-row
   term in rev C is real and the matrix path removes it.
2. Materializing A in DRAM is NOT viable at decode footprints: at E=10 the 62.5 MiB stream costs
   ~145 us on its own (~360-390 GB/s), which is why rev D loses below rows 6 there. A shipping kernel
   MUST assemble A from trellis bytes in registers/SLM. This probe deliberately did not test that.
3. Therefore step 2 is worth building, with a specific prize: at E=10/rows=4 the ceiling implied by
   combining rev D's flat curve with the trellis's own 5.86 MiB read is roughly 20-30 us against rev
   C's 112.5 us. The whole of that prize rests on assembly costing less than ~2 slots per weight,
   which is the number step 2 must report first, before any rate.

## Is rev D actually byte-bound? (marginal cost, not assertion)

Saying "145 us of A streaming" is an interpretation; the test is to grow A and watch the slope. rev D at
rows=4, KS=4, A = 6.25 MiB per expert:

    E     A MiB       us    us/MiB   implied GB/s
    1      6.25     25.7      4.11          255.0
    2     12.50     34.7      2.78          377.7
    4     25.00     55.1      2.20          475.8
    5     31.25     81.5      2.61          402.1
   10     62.50    147.5      2.36          444.3

Marginal from E=1 to E=10: (147.5 - 25.7) us over 56.25 MiB = **2.17 us/MiB = 484 GB/s**, about 72% of
the measured 674 GB/s copy peak - so the incremental cost is genuinely A streaming, and the intercept is
about 12 us of row-independent, byte-independent fixed cost (the matrix path itself, partial writes,
setup). At E=1 the fixed part dominates and effective GB/s is pessimistic, which is exactly why the
small-E rows curves are flat.

What that implies for step 2, stated as arithmetic rather than hope: replacing A's 4 B/weight with the
trellis's 0.375 B/weight removes ~135 us of the ~147 us at E=10/rows=4, leaving the ~12 us fixed cost
plus whatever the unpack-and-assemble loop adds. So the ceiling for a real DPAS kernel at that shape is
tens of microseconds against rev C's 112.5 us, and the *only* question is whether assembling A costs more
than it saves - which is the number step 2 must print first.

## Cross-check: three independent implementations agree bit for bit

Second configuration, different split and row count (E=2, KS=2, rows=4, A = 12.5 MiB):

    revD(dpas, KS=2, rows=4) bit-exact vs y_ref: True
    revB still bit-exact on the same case:       True
    revD == revB:                                True

That is the NumPy reference, rev B's scalar dp4a loop, and rev D's XMX integer matrix path producing
identical float32 output from identical checkpoint bytes. It also exercises a different KS, so the
partial-sum tree differs by construction and the integer accumulator is confirmed order-independent
across the two paths - which is what makes the DPAS variant substitutable inside the engine's
completion protocol rather than a second numerics regime to validate separately.

## Step 2 (rev E, probe_dpas2.cpp): A assembled from trellis bytes, no byte expansion

Same case, same epilogue, but the kernel now reads ONLY the trellis bytes (5.86 MiB) and builds the A
operand itself: 16 lanes unpack a tile with rev C's validated window math, store each window's four
product bytes as one u32, then four load/mad pairs consume the tile (2 column-blocks x 2 byte-blocks).
B is the activation replicated 4x along K (4k x 16 tokens = 163 KB, L2-resident), staged once per
k-slice and reused across every expert so it is not re-read per expert.

The sourcing of the two operands turned out to matter more than anything else - a 2x2 factorial at
rows=4, KS=8:

    A source        B source       us    slots/weight
    SLM             SLM          372.1        32.5
    SLM             global       191.5        16.7
    global scratch  SLM          456.5        39.9
    global scratch  global       109.1         9.5   <- and bit-exact vs y_ref

Feeding joint_matrix_load from LOCAL (SLM) space costs about 20 slots per weight; the same operand
sourced from global space costs about 2. That is a 3.4x difference on the whole kernel, and it is a
property of the lowering, not of the algorithm: the intuitive design (stage the assembled tile in SLM,
which is fast memory and right next to the math unit) is the slow one here. A 1 KB global scratch region
per sub-group, rewritten every tile, stays in L2 and costs no DRAM traffic at all - it reports 56 GB/s
against the trellis, far below the 292 GB/s the GEMV achieves.

rev E (global scratch A, global B) against rev C, min of 4 interleaved runs, E=10 gate_proj:

    KS   rows |  revC us |  revE us  slots/w | revE vs revC
     8     1 |     75.6 |    153.3     13.4 | x0.49
     8     4 |    112.5 |    109.5      9.6 | x1.03
     8     8 |    252.0 |    113.3      9.9 | x2.22
     8    16 |    488.3 |    122.2     10.7 | x4.00
    16     1 |     75.3 |     95.5      8.3 | x0.79
    16     4 |    112.6 |     99.8      8.7 | x1.13
    16     8 |    251.9 |    108.8      9.5 | x2.32
    16    16 |    484.7 |    127.2     11.1 | x3.81

  best per rows:  rows 1 x0.79 | rows 4 x1.13 | rows 8 x2.31 | rows 16 x3.98

WHAT THIS SETTLES
* The row term is genuinely removable: rev E spends 8.3 - 11.1 slots/weight across rows 1..16 (flat),
  where rev C spends 7.0 at rows=1 and 31 at rows=16. Predicted crossover from the fitted model was
  rows ~2.4; measured is ~3.5, so the model slightly flattered DPAS.
* Assembly is NOT free and this is the number step 2 existed to produce: ~8.5 slots/weight all-in for
  rev E versus ~5.8 for rev C's unpack-only fixed cost, i.e. assembling A and driving the matrix unit
  costs roughly 2.7 slots/weight more than the dp4a path's own fixed work. Because it is a FIXED cost,
  it is repaid once per weight instead of once per token - which is the entire argument for the matrix
  path, and it pays back from rows ~4.
* Consequence for the engine: two decode schedules keyed on rows - dp4a (rev C) at rows <= 2, DPAS
  (rev E) at rows >= 4. Same integer accumulator, so both are bit-exact against the same reference and
  they agree with each other bit-for-bit; the split is a performance decision, not a numerics regime.
* Caveats, stated plainly: rev E is a first cut and unoptimized (matrix objects re-created inside the
  inner loop, two barriers per tile, no double-buffering, scratch geometry untuned), so the x8/x16
  numbers should be read as "a working DPAS path already wins this much" rather than as the ceiling.
  Measured on ONE geometry (E=10, gate_proj, k=2560, n=640, K=3) - the other eight checkpoints,
  including GLM's n=4096/tn=256 and Qwen3.5/3.6's tn=32, are unmeasured, and rows=1 losing means the
  engine carries two code paths that both need Gate A in CI.

## Was "prepare the operands once at load time" measured? Yes - it is rev D, and it loses

rev D *is* the prepare-once design: A pre-expanded in DRAM so the kernel's operand-assembly cost is
literally zero. Swept to its best split-K (min of 3, burst mode) against rev E's best and rev C:

    rows | revD best (assembly FREE, bytes 10.7x) | revE best (assembly on the fly) | rev C
       1 |                     135.8 us (ks=1)    |      95.5 us                    |  76.8 us
       4 |                     137.2 us (ks=1)    |      99.8 us                    | 112.5 us
       8 |                     138.5 us (ks=1)    |     108.8 us                    | 251.8 us
      16 |                     141.8 us (ks=1)    |     122.2 us                    | 483.7 us

rev E beats the free-assembly version at every row count (x1.16 to x1.42), because rev D is entirely
byte-bound: 62.5 MiB of A at rev D's own measured 484 GB/s marginal is 135.4 us, versus 135.8 us
degistered at rows=1 - the math is completely hidden under the streaming. Even at the 674 GB/s copy
peak the floor is 97.2 us, which still loses to rev E everywhere and to rev C at rows=1.

Note also what rev E already is: the unpack runs ONCE per weight per launch regardless of rows - that is
precisely why its curve is flat. So preparing at load time buys no extra amortization; it removes only
the ~5.8-slot unpack and charges 10.7x bytes for it. Capacity makes that unaffordable: 4 B/weight turns
Qwen3.8@2.68 from 36.33 GiB into ~388 GiB against 32,656 MiB of VRAM, collapsing the resident expert
share from h ~ 0.98 to ~ 0.08 and inverting the whole placement plan; a decoded *cache* is no better,
since L2 is 24.0 MiB (measured) and holds ~3.8 experts of expanded bytes. Cheaper prepared forms exist
(10-bit decoded values need >= 1.25 B/weight, e.g. a u4+u8 plane split at 1.5 B/weight with two mads,
still exact) but 3.3-4x bytes still cuts h to ~0.25.

The idea that survives is not expansion but a **same-size repack**: the trellis packs 8 windows into
3K bits, so a lane's windows straddle dword boundaries and cost 3 strided loads plus shifts. Reordering
the ring within a tile - still K bits per weight, zero extra bytes, zero extra DRAM - so one wide load
covers a lane's windows attacks the ~5.8 fixed slots instead of the ~2.7 assembly slots. If the fixed
cost fell to ~3, rev E would cost ~5.5 slots/weight and beat rev C at rows=1 too, collapsing the engine
to one decode schedule. Price: the B70's bytes stop being interchangeable with the 3090/CPU trellis
kernels, plus a load-time pass - the E4c trade-off, so it must clear that bar. Gate E28.

## Would compile-time K help rev E too? Priced, and the answer is "a little"

Same knobs, rev E at its best split-K (KS=16, A in L2 scratch, B global), min of 3:

  rows | full | unpack+assemble only | matrix path only | full - matrix-only | predicted with x1.90 extraction
     1 | 95.5 |                69.3  |        41.5      |   26.2 us (27%)      |  83.1 us  (x1.15)
     4 | 99.7 |                74.7  |        57.0      |   25.0 us (25%)      |  87.9 us  (x1.13)
     8 |127.2 |                83.2  |        55.8      |   44.0 us (35%)      | 106.4 us  (x1.20)
    16 |127.8 |               101.5  |        73.3      |   26.3 us (21%)      | 115.3 us  (x1.11)

Two caveats stated plainly. (1) The halves do not sum (69.3 + 41.5 > 95.5): each knob removes work but
leaves the loop, barrier and store scaffolding in place, so the difference bounds the extraction share
rather than measuring it exactly. (2) Applying rev C's measured x1.90 to that whole share overstates the
win, because K-constancy folds shifts and the straddle select only - it does nothing for the u32 stores
into the A scratch, the two barriers per tile, the matrix loads or the PART stores, which is where most
of rev E's overhead lives.

And rev E has a prerequisite rev C does not. Its lane mapping is `t = lane + 16*tt`, so the intra-dword
offset is `(K*(8*lane + 1) - 16) & 31` - **lane-dependent** (it is invariant only when K*8*lane is a
multiple of 32 for every lane, i.e. K=4). rev C got the win for free because its mapping is
`t = 4*q0 + a4`, where the `K*32*q0` term vanishes mod 32. So rev E needs a lane remap first: keep `a4`
as the unrolled inner index and split `j` across lanes, folding the j-half offset into a per-lane bit
base (one variable shift to form the 64-bit base per tile, then compile-time shifts for the windows).

Verdict: worth doing only as a by-product - the engine will template K for rev C anyway, and sharing one
extractor between the dp4a and DPAS paths removes a second copy of the trickiest inner loop. On its own
merits, x1.1-x1.2 does not justify the remap; rev E's bigger levers are the per-tile barrier pair and the
A scratch round trip itself, which only ESIMD register fragments (xmx::dpas on simd operands) can remove -
this toolchain has no joint_matrix_construct, so the API forces the memory round trip.

## Reproduce

    python3 dump_gemv_case.py /tmp/gemv_dpE10 10 16 0 --with-reference --with-abytes
    ./probe_dpas /tmp/gemv_dpE10 21 <rows> <KS>        # KS in {1,2,4,8}; KS*2*32 must divide 4k
    ./probe_gemv /tmp/gemv_dpE10 0 21 <rows> 1 4       # rev C on identical bytes for comparison

    # rev E: A assembled from trellis bytes; the sourcing flags are the 2x2 experiment above
    DPAS_A_GLOBAL=1 DPAS_B_GLOBAL=1 ./probe_dpas2 /tmp/gemv_dpE10 21 <rows> <ks>
    # devinfo: cache hierarchy measured (global 32656 MiB, SLM 128 KiB/group, L2 24 MiB, 256 CUs)
    icpx -fsycl -O2 -o /tmp/devinfo devinfo.cpp && /tmp/devinfo
    DPAS_NO_MAD=1 ./probe_dpas2 ...       # unpack + assembly only (prices the assembly)
    DPAS_NO_UNPACK=1 ./probe_dpas2 ...    # matrix path only
    DPAS_NO_UNPACK=1 ./probe_dpas2 ...    # matrix path only
