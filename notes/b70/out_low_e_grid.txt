Low-E grid (E = 2, 4): fusion pays MORE as the grid empties, and the real rule couples fusion with split-K
============================================================================================================
The last corner of the E-grid gap ("X/EPT=1 was validated at E=10"). Cases dumped at E=2 and E=4 (gate + up, K=3,
same tk/tn as everything else), rev L (/tmp/revL9), rows 1/8/16 and, at rows=1, KS = 1/2/4.

WINDOW NOTE, CORRECTED AFTER THE FACT: the anchor read 44.0 us at the start of this batch and 25.8 us at the end,
and I blamed the browser. That was wrong - it was an under-warmed anchor: 200 reps is 3.8 ms of work and leaves
the device at 1200 MHz (44.0 us), while 2000 reps reaches 2000 MHz and 19.2 us. The probes themselves ran with
WARM_S=2 of executed work, so the absolute us below are probably sound; what remains true regardless is that the
conclusions were drawn from ratios between adjacent runs of the same cell.
Everything read below is a RATIO measured between adjacent runs of the same cell, which is the comparison this
box can still support.

1. One expert per thread is fine at low E too (the D4 question). E=2 and E=4 behave like scaled versions of E=10;
   nothing about the layout breaks when the grid is only 320-640 threads. The concern that motivated this batch -
   that a tiny grid might make the expert-grid port pathological - did not materialise.

2. Fusion pays more the emptier the device is, exactly as D8 predicts: 1.78-1.85x at E=2/4 rows 8/16 and rows=1
   KS=1, against 1.35-1.63x at E=10 and ~1.0x at E=96. Fusion is a parallelism purchase, and the parallelism is
   scarcer at low E.

3. THE NEW PART - fusion and split-K are not either/or. At E=2 the best schedule is fused AND KS=4 (9.7 us),
   not fused KS=1 (17.6) and not separate KS=4 (14.1): split-K buys 2.3x at E=2 while fusion buys a further 1.45x
   on top. The E25a phrasing ("they are substitutes, so fuse at KS=1") was an E=10 artifact: at E=10, KS=2 fused
   cost +7%, because KS=1 fused had ALREADY filled the grid.

4. What actually predicts all of it is a target grid occupancy, reached from either lever:
      threads = tiles_n_effective x KS x 2 x E        (tiles_n_effective = tn separate, 2*tn fused)
   every measured optimum lands in the 1280-1600 band (~0.6-0.8 x 2048 slots):
      E=2  fused KS=4 -> 1280  (best, 9.7)     E=2  fused KS=2 -> 640   (under-filled, 11.3)
      E=4  fused KS=2 -> 1280  (best, 12.1)    E=4  fused KS=4 -> 2560  (over-subscribed, 14.8)
      E=10 fused KS=1 -> 1600  (best, 20.7)    E=10 fused KS=2 -> 3200  (+7%, measured in E25a)
      E=96 separate KS=1 -> 7680 (already 3.75 waves; neither lever helps, out_egrid.txt)
   Restated rule (amends D8): choose KS and fusion JOINTLY so the gemm grid lands in ~0.6-0.8 x HW_THREADS,
   preferring fusion when both fillers achieve the same occupancy, since it also removes a launch and gives
   combine_both its single-combine schedule. Below the band, raise KS before considering fusion; above it, stop
   splitting and consider unfusing.

5. Rows behaves as expected (per-expert cost rises slowly with rows at fixed E), and gate/up remain symmetric
   (16.3/16.3, 10.0/9.9, 20.5/20.1), which is the null check that the fused grid's projection split is balanced.

6. CORRECTNESS at low E: the fused outputs left on disk are bit-exact for BOTH projections at E=2 and E=4
   (gate vs the gate reference, `_p2` vs the up reference). Checked from files, no device work. Note the trap I
   walked into on the first pass: comparing the gate dir's `_p2` against the *gate* directory's `y_ref.npy` reports
   WRONG, because `_p2` is the up projection's result. Any fused-output comparison must pair each buffer with the
   reference of the tensor it actually is - which is why `gate_dpas.py` keeps the two refs explicit instead of
   globbing a directory.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT YET CHANGE IN THE PLANNERS. plan_decode_cost.py collapses each family's table to "best KS per
rows" at E=10 and exposes should_fuse(tn, KS, E), which encodes the old exclusive form of the rule. Implementing
rule 4 needs the per-KS cost table (currently not stored: KS is optimised away inside the measurement), so the
change is: measure and store us per (expert, rows, KS) for the fused and separate schedules at one E, then let the
planner pick the (fusion, KS) pair that lands nearest the band without exceeding it. Doing that under a
contaminated window would bake the contamination into the model, so it waits for an anchor-healthy batch.
UPDATE, same day: that reason evaporated. The window was healthy and the anchor check was under-warmed, so the
per-KS table can be built whenever the runs are available - the remaining obstacle is effort, not contamination.
That is the useful kind of outcome from a false alarm: the follow-up is unblocked rather than merely deferred.
