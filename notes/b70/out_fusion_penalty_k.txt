How deep is the fusion penalty, and where does it start? Same E ladder at three bit rates
===========================================================================================
Task set by out_ks_grid.txt: the L2 footprint argument predicts WHERE fusion should stop paying (2·E·b vs L2) but
not how DEEP the penalty is - 1.27x at K=3 and 1.79x at K=6 were observed at the same 37.4 MiB live footprint.
This measures both directions, and in doing so refutes part of the rule I had already shipped.

Design note (my first attempt at this experiment was wrong): holding 2·E·b fixed across K means varying E, but
fill also depends on E (grid = 2·tn·KS·2·E with tn identical across quants), so a constant-footprint sweep
confounds bytes with occupancy. Instead: fix the E ladder and vary K, so fill is identical cell-for-cell and only
bytes-per-weight and unpack density change.

All cells rows=1, min of 2 interleaved passes, rev L (/tmp/revL9 via sweep_ks_grid.py), fused cells bit-exact for
both projections, anchors 19.0-19.8 us (healthy; 19.8 is one sample). Gain = separate/fused, so >1 means FUSED is
better. Gains are computed at matched KS, which is the only comparison that isolates the fusion lever.

WHAT THE DATA SAYS

1. Depth scales with bits-per-weight, sub-linearly. Worst penalty per K: K=2 = 1.16x worse (0.866), K=3 = 1.27x
   (0.79), K=6 = 1.88x (0.532). Bytes differ 3x across that range, depth only ~1.6x, so the depth is not a direct
   function of traffic. Consistent with "a miss costs more when more arithmetic is exposed behind it" (denser
   trellis = more unpack per byte), but the exponent is not established.

2. Onset is KS-DEPENDENT, which the footprint model cannot express. At K=3, E=16: separate wins at KS=1 (0.81)
   and fused wins at KS=2 (1.29) - same cell, same bytes, opposite sign, because the two choices put 1280 and
   2560 items on the device. Any single-number "fusion threshold in MiB" is therefore over-simplified; the honest
   statement is that bytes shift the whole curve earlier as they grow (K=6 is penalty-from-E=16-onward at KS=1,
   K=3 from E=16/20, K=2 not until ~E=48), but the sign at a given cell also turns on the fill that cell produces.

3. THE FOOTPRINT RULE IS NOT A SWITCH - and I had shipped it as one. Two independent counterexamples: K=2 at
   E=32 sits inside the predicted band (fused 25.0 MiB > L2, one stream 12.5 fits) and fusion WON by 1.32x; and
   K=3 at E=16 flips sign with KS. So best_launch() now ANNOTATES ("cache warning ... the sign near the edges is
   not predictable from bytes alone") and only excludes fused candidates when the caller passes
   enforce_cache_rule=True, which should be set only where a measurement backs it. The selftest pins all three
   behaviours: warns by default, keeps fused candidates by default, excludes when told, and stays silent well
   past L2.

4. An unexplained shape worth recording: at K=6 the fused cost is almost E-independent (4.475 / 4.479 / 4.456 us
   per pair at E=20/24/32) while separate scales steeply (2.38 -> 4.64). A schedule whose cost barely responds to
   a 1.6x change in work is pinned by something other than its own traffic - plausibly the two concurrent weight
   streams inside one launch meeting a queue/row limit rather than an L2 capacity limit.

FOR THE PLANNERS AND THE ENGINE
  * Decide from the measured tables. The byte test is a flag reading "near a known penalty band; sign not
    modelled here", nothing more.
  * Where the dense recipe matters, prefer separate launches from E ~ 16 upward at K=6: the penalty there is both
    deepest and earliest, and 6.05 bpw is exactly where a fill-driven argument would otherwise tempt fusion.
  * Still open: rows > 1 at K=2/K=6 (this file is rows=1), so "the band narrows as rows grows" remains a
    prediction. Note the KS-dependence in point 2 when designing that sweep - it needs both KS values per cell,
    not one.
