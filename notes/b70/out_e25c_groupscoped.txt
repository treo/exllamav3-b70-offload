E25c revisited: can the three-projection fusion be delivered WITHOUT a device-wide barrier?
=============================================================================================
Asked after out_e25c_barrier.txt rejected E25c on a 7.6 us grid-wide barrier. The question is right: that barrier
was a consequence of the MAPPING I assumed, not of the goal. Down needs the whole intermediate row, and the row
is produced by 40 column-tile items - but "40 items" is small. It could fit inside one work-group, in which case
the sync is a work-group barrier, which is hardware and legal without any residency gamble.

Short answer: **yes, it is expressible - and it is still the wrong trade, for a different and better reason than
before.** Four measurements, all in anchor-healthy windows (19.1-19.3 us).

1. THE SYNC IS NOT THE PROBLEM (probe_groupfused.cpp, 2048 items, 8 rounds, matched bodies, min of 5)

     grid-wide spin barrier, 2048 work-items        +7.58 us per barrier
     work-group barrier, wg = 64..1024              -0.03 .. +0.22 us  -> below the probe's resolution
     work-group barrier + global/L2 hand-off        ~ 0
     device local memory: 128 KiB per group, max work-group 1024 items

   So the coordination primitive that killed E25c costs ~7.6 us only because it spans the device. Confined to a
   group it is free, and the intermediate (h = rows x 640 = 2.5 KiB at rows=1, 40 KiB at rows=16) fits in SLM
   with room to spare.

2. BUT THE BACKEND LIMITS THIS KERNEL TO 64 WORK-ITEMS PER GROUP. Forcing more fails outright:
       "Total number of work-items in a work-group cannot exceed 64 for this kernel"
   (device-wise max_work_group_size says 1024; the ESIMD path caps it at 64, and 8 hardware threads per EU means
   a group of 64 already spans EUs, which is where the "group barrier is local and cheap" intuition starts to
   weaken.) An expert's dependency closure is 40 gate tiles + 40 up tiles + 160 down tiles, so a group can hold
   the *gate/up half* (40 items, each doing both projections' tile) and must then chew down's 160 tiles at 2.5
   items each - possible inside 64, with no slack at all.

3. RE-GROUPING PER SE IS CHEAP (DPAS_WG added to probe_dpas9: same grid, same work, larger groups; WARM_S=2,
   min of 2 passes; anchor 19.1 before / 19.3 after):

     case                 wg=2    wg=4    wg=8   wg=16   wg=32   wg=64
     E10 r1  fused       20.80   20.70   21.00   22.60   22.60   22.80     (+10% at wg=64)
     E10 r16 fused       33.60   33.80   34.00   35.00   35.30   34.90     (+4%)
     E32 r1  fused       83.50   84.40   84.70   85.30   86.50   88.20     (+6%)
     E10 r1  separate    16.80   16.80   16.80   17.50   17.70    n/a      (800 items / 64 does not divide)

   Groups of 8 - exactly one EU - are free (+1%). So "big groups are slow" is NOT the objection either.

4. THE REAL OBJECTION IS THE ONE THIS CHAPTER KEEPS HITTING: CONCURRENCY. One group per expert means the device
   runs E groups. At E=10 that is 640 items in flight instead of 1600 - a 2.5x loss of fill, in the regime where
   fill is what binds. The cost of losing fill is already measured, not guessed: the same gate+up work at 800
   items (separate, KS=1) takes 33.7 us against 20.8 us at 1600 items (fused) - +62%. Route 1 would start from
   640 items, i.e. worse than that, to save two launches worth ~6 us on a ~36 us layer. Even granting the +10%
   grouping cost and nothing else, it is a multiple-of-six loss.

   And the fix for fill is the wrong direction: restoring 2048 items by holding ~32 experts resident on the
   device means ~2.6 MiB x 32 = 83 MiB of weights on the B70, which stops being an offload accelerator for a
   *subset* of experts and starts being a full-resident card.

ALTERNATIVE ROUTE 3, considered and dismissed on arithmetic (not measured): make the hand-off unnecessary by
having each group accumulate its own slice of down's contribution directly (h_slice x Wd[slice, :] -> partial y).
No barrier at any level. But that writes a full-width y partial per slice: 40 slices x rows x 2560 x 4 B =
410 KiB per expert per layer at rows=1, against ~5 KiB of h today - an 80x partial-traffic explosion, which is
the E7 item-5 problem this chapter already solved structurally by keeping one expert per thread.

REVISED DECISION (supersedes the wording in D10)
  E25c stays rejected, but the reason is not "a device-wide barrier is required" - a group-scoped design exists,
  its sync is free, its SLM fits, and large groups are only ~10% expensive. It is rejected because the
  dependency closure per expert (>= 40 items, <= 64 by backend limit) forces one group per expert, and that
  collapses in-flight work from 1600 to 640 items at E=10 in exactly the small-E, rows=1 regime where fill
  decides everything (fill loss measured at +62% going 1600 -> 800; prize ~6 us of launches). The barrier was an
  incidental cost of my first mapping; the *granularity* is the inherent cost.

  What would change the answer: a geometry where experts-per-launch is large (E >= ~32 so E*64 items >= 2048) AND
  the device is not fill-limited. Checked against data already in hand rather than left as an invitation: at
  rows=16 the fill penalty is smaller but still dominant - the same gate+up work costs 3.37 us/pair at 1600 items
  and 4.58 us/pair at 800 (out_ks_grid.txt), i.e. +36% instead of the +62% at rows=1, which on a ~50 us layer is
  ~18 us lost to save ~6 us of launches. So route 1 is negative at rows=1 AND rows=16, and the regime where it
  could break even (fill no longer binding) is beyond the 16-row bucket the engine splits on anyway - i.e. not a
  regime this design will operate in. E25c is closed, not deferred.
