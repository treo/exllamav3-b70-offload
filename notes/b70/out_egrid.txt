=============================================================================
Why: every decode number in this chapter was measured on E=10 case dirs. D4 (one expert per thread) and D8
(fuse gate+up when the separate launches under-fill the device) are both stated in terms of grid occupancy, so
both are E-dependent claims that had never been tested off E=10. Cases: Qwen3.8 gate/up, K=3, tk=160, tn=40,

WHAT IT MEANS

1. One expert per thread (D4) scales cleanly with E. Single-projection gate time: 16.9 us at E=10, 40.0 at E=32
   (2.4x for 3.2x the experts), 120.0 at E=96 (7.1x for 9.6x) at rows=1/KS=1. No grid pathology, no cliff: the
   extra experts simply add threads, and the device chews through 1.25-7.5 waves without incident.

2. D8 survives where it matters and is imprecise exactly where precision is worthless. Every cell where the rule
   says "fuse" (grid 800, E=10 KS=1) fuses by 1.35-1.63x. Every cell where the grid is already >= 1.25 waves
   either ties (0.96-1.00x against fusing) or loses by <= 6%. So the 0.6*HW_THREADS threshold should be read as
   "the point past which fusion stops buying anything", not as "past which fusion hurts": the three rule breaks
   are all fusion winning by 3-5%, i.e. harmless to have predicted wrong about.
   Amended rule (close-out D8): fuse when the separate launches under-fill the device; beyond that the choice is
   a wash within +-6%, so pick on launch-count grounds (fewer launches is preferable, and combine_both already
   depends on the fused gemm having produced both partial regions).

3. Efficiency falls with rows at large E, as the cost model says it should: E=96 rows=1 KS=1 runs 2x56.2 MiB in
   240.7 us = 492 GB/s, while E=96 rows=16 KS=2 runs the same bytes in 413.3 us = 287 GB/s. The arithmetic
   (rows of DPAS per tile) overtakes the weight stream, which is the same transition that motivated rev J's
   one-expert-per-thread layout at small E.

maybe it was warming the device and the honest 1 s is too short). Sweeping WARM_S on the corrected loop,
min of 2 reps each:

  E96 fused rows16 ks2:  0s 404.3 | 1s 410.1 | 2s 409.9 | 3s 409.9 | 5s 410.9 | 10s 410.1 | 20s 412.6 us
  E10 fused rows16 ks1:  0s  37.5 | 1s  33.5 | 2s  33.5 | 3s  33.5 | 5s  33.8 | 10s  33.6 | 20s  33.8 us

No trend at E=96 (1.4% spread, unwarmed marginally the fastest - within noise, and no interpretation is
warranted from a 1.4% span), and at E=10 the familiar picture: +12% when unwarmed, then flat from 1 s to 20 s,
which reproduces the original 0/1/3/5/20 s calibration under the new definition. Two reasons this is reassuring
rather than lucky: the corrected loop really does execute WARM_S seconds of work (wall time tracks it 1:1 in the
table above), and the timing loop takes min-of-4-rounds AFTER the earliest rounds have already ramped the clock,
so the estimator is insensitive to warm-up length as long as several rounds run. What WARM_S still buys is that
the FIRST timed round is not thrown away on a downclocked device - so keep it at 1-3 s, but do not treat it as
the thing that makes numbers comparable.

Anchor during this sub-experiment: 24.7 us (unhealthy), which is the contamination described next - absolute us
here are pessimistic; the 0s-vs-20s *differences* are what is being read.

CAVEAT - SUPERSEDED (same day): the low anchor readings below were an under-warmed anchor, not contamination.
Re-measuring the anchor with 6000 reps in this kind of window gives 19.1 us at 2150 MHz, while 120-200 reps gives
43-44 us at 1200 MHz because 2-4 ms of work never raises the clock (LESSONS 54's correction has the table). So
this batch's ABSOLUTE us stand - the probes themselves ran with WARM_S >= 1 s of executed work. The paragraph is
kept because the co-tenant is real and the inference was reasonable, but its attribution is wrong.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The anchor (rev I, gate rows=1, an unchanged binary whose healthy value is 19.4 us) read 22.9 us after this sweep
and 20.6-23.1 us across six interleaved samples, with one 43.7 us outlier. That is not thermal soak from the run:
`fuser -v /dev/dri/renderD128` shows **firefox holding the same render node** as these probes (RSS 1.75 GB, plus
isolated web-content processes, plus the niri Wayland compositor on the card). Every number in this chapter was
therefore measured on a GPU shared with a desktop browser.

Consequences, stated plainly:
  * absolute us in the table above are probably 6-19% pessimistic and must not be compared with published values
    from an anchor-healthy window;
  * within-cell ratios (fused vs separate, g vs u) remain valid because both arms were measured back to back in
    the same window, which is the discipline the whole chapter has relied on;
  * the historical cross-window swings that motivated "never compare absolute us" (once measured at 2.3x) now
    have a plausible mechanism: browser activity, not just clock state.
  * Before trusting an absolute number, check the anchor AND the render-node users:
      fuser -v /dev/dri/renderD128
      DPAS_X=1 ./probe_dpas6 /tmp/gemv_dpE10 6000 1 2     # ~19.1-19.4 us; >=2000 reps needed to ramp the clock
    A depressed anchor is evidence about the ANCHOR first: it needs >= 2000 reps (~40 ms of work) to ramp, and
    read xpu-smi's frequency before concluding anything about the device or its neighbours.
  * Proper fix, not attempted here: run timing batches from a session without a compositor/browser on that card
    (VT switch, headless runner, or a second GPU). Until then the chapter's absolute figures carry a shared-device
    asterisk.

HARNESS DEFECTS FOUND WHILE DOING THIS (both fixed in probe_dpas9.cpp)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
a) Double free in teardown. `sycl::free(Y,...); sycl::free(PART,...)` appeared twice (my own edit duplicated a
   line I had not removed), so every rev L binary freed two buffers twice. It raised
   UR_RESULT_ERROR_INVALID_MEM_OBJECT from sycl::free at process exit - SIGABRT, rc=134 - and because stdout was
   block-buffered into a pipe, the aborted run LOST ITS TIMING LINE. Measurement drivers took min-of-N over the
   runs that printed, so killed rounds vanished silently: 27 core dumps from /tmp/revL7 against the handful of
   missing cells I noticed. Latent because the second free only throws when nothing reuses the address in
   between, which is why it hit some shapes and not others and looked data-dependent.
b) Warm-up enqueued without ever waiting. `do { dispatch(); } while (wall < WARM_S)` submits until wall time
   elapses, then the first q.wait() drains the backlog. At E=10 host submit (~40 us) and device work (~40 us)
   match, so it is invisible. At E=96 a dispatch costs ~400 us of GPU time per ~40 us of submit, so a nominal
   1 s warm-up queued ~30 s of work: E=96 fused runs took 44.2 s wall for 1.0 s of intended warm-up (measured
   twice: 44.2, 44.0), and this is why a 54-run sweep "went just through the first for loop" in 50 minutes.
   Fixed by waiting every 8 dispatches, which makes WARM_S mean "seconds of work executed". Result: E=96 fused
   44.2 s -> 1.6 s wall, with the measured kernel time unchanged (402.9 us -> 410.0 us, within noise).

Also worth noting on method: the fix in (a) was located by reading the surviving outputs and the core dump
(`main -> sycl::free -> usm::free -> throw`) rather than by re-running anything - 26 outputs on disk were
certified bit-exact first (all six E=96 fused configurations, E=32 fused rows=16, and the singles), which
answered the E!=10 correctness question without a single device run.
