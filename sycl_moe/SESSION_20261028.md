## Finding 1 — the prefill plateau is NOT the link (refutes the NOTES state)

Default regime (`-mcs 256 -mcpa`, auto `stream_t` = 11), one 4096-row prefill, 2.02 s:

| resource | measured |
|---|---|
| 3090 compute stream busy | **1648 ms = 81%** of span (18.5k kernels) |
| 3090 copy stream busy | 475 ms = 22% (5.1k pinned->device memcpys, 92.8 us each = one 1.17 MB expert at 12.6 GB/s) |
| UNION of both streams | 1961 ms = **91.4%** busy; only ~66 ms of real idle (the 119 ms "gap" is a trace preamble artefact) |
| PCIe link during 6 reps | mean **3.2 GB/s**, peak 8.2 GB/s (wire capacity 12.8 GB/s each way, measured by copy test) |
| B70 during prefill | compute util **21-28%**, **70 W of 275 W**, mem-BW 2% |
| 3090 main-thread CPU | 1.90 s self-CPU per 2.05 s wall (`aten::copy_` CPU-total 1.57 s / 7.6k calls) |

and the B70 does *nothing during that forward*. But that is only the FIRST forward: the placement
is dynamic (promote-on-miss), and by the timed reps the worker tier has shrunk to the cold experts
(`split_report.py` fwd4-6: ~5.0-7.0 k assignments/layer instead of 20.8 k, ~100 streamed experts,
~330 tail picks). Corrected steady-state picture, which is the one that matters:

| resource (steady state, default flags) | measured |
|---|---|
| 3090 compute stream | 1649 ms busy of a 2159 ms span; UNION of streams 1963 ms = 91% |
| 3090 streamed-MoE kernels | `exl3_moe_kernel<…,64>` 424 ms + `<…,32>` 42 + `<…,16>` 31 + `reconstruct_had_batch` 40 + gather 25 + pageable H2D 38 = **~600 ms** |
| 3090 main-model kernels (unchanged by any offload knob) | f16acc gemms 392, qsa 108, gr_dots/gr_gate 173, hc_apply 45, GDN/conv ~60 = **~1.05 s floor** |
| copy stream (weight DMA) | 475 ms = 22% of span (5.2 k pinned→device copies) |
| B70 | 592 ms/rep of plugin time for ~16 k tail picks (**~37 us per cold pick**, i.e. ~24 us per nearly-empty chunk) at 21-28% util, 70 W |

So 'prefill is link-bound at ~2000 tok/s' is wrong for the current build: the 3090 is ~91% busy,
the link is at a quarter of its rate, and the B70 - which already holds every worker expert at
608 GB/s - spends half a second shredding fragments for 3% of the picks. Other corrections to
NOTES: `expert_bytes` is **1 228 800 B**, not the 2.46 MB quoted in the prefill-economics section
(one expert DMA is ~92 us, not 184 us), and `cap_rows` was declared 'flat' by sweeping a regime
where `n_tail ≈ 0` - it cannot matter there by construction.

## Finding 2 — the B70 leg is chunk-fullness-limited, and `cap_rows` is the knob

Cost model (from `moe_layout.hpp`): a DPAS chunk = <=8 **same-expert** token slots and costs
~11.8 us in fragments whether full or not, so B70 us/pick = 11.8 / (picks per chunk). Chunks are
built per *(expert x cap_rows job)*, so a small `cap_rows` shreds an expert's picks into
partially-filled chunks. Measured on the model, all-worker-work-on-B70 (`STREAM_T=100000`):

| arm | prefill 4096 | worker/b70 report |
|---|---|---|
| cap_rows 128 (default) | 1411 (1344-1475) | 1.4 ms/job, **18 us/row**, 78 rows/job avg |
| cap_rows 1024 | **1568** (1508-1645) | 6.2 ms/job, **7 us/row**, 640 rows/job |

+11% from `cap_rows` alone, in a regime where the earlier sweep saw nothing. At 5 picks/row the
1024-row job is ~1.4 us/pick (near the 11.8/8 model); the residual (~2.5 us/pick end to end) is
per-job and activation traffic. Implication: to buy B70 time for the 3090, the tail must be
delivered as **few big jobs**, which the default `cap_rows=128` prevents.

## Finding 4 — measured policy curve (interleaved `ab.sh`, min-of-2-rounds, 3 reps each)

`ab.sh st_fine`, lens 1024/2048/4096 (round-robin per rep so drift hits every arm):

| arm | 1024 | 2048 | 4096 |
|---|---|---|---|
| default (`stream_t` auto≈11, `cap_rows` 128) | 1260 | 1651 | 1983 |
| `STREAM_T=16 SLOT_ROWS=2048` | 1325 | 1697 | 2006 |
| `STREAM_T=24 SLOT_ROWS=2048` | 1261 | **1780** | 2048 |
| `STREAM_T=32 SLOT_ROWS=2048` | 1271 | 1700 | 2049 |
| `STREAM_T=24 SLOT_ROWS=1024` | **1332** | 1728 | **2081** |

+4…6% at every length from a pure policy change (leave the coldest experts to the B70, and give
the B70 its tail as few large jobs instead of 128-row shreds). Round-to-round arm spread was
±2-3%, and the winner took both rounds, so the effect is outside noise.

### Why the old sweep saw nothing

`NOTES.md` records 'stream_t 4/11/16/24/32/48/64/96 is FLAT 11..48' and 'cap_rows … FLAT'. Both
were measured with `cap_rows=128` (default): the tail is then split into 128-row jobs, an expert's
counted picks are shredded across jobs, and every chunk is nearly empty, so the B70 charges
~11.8-24 us for 1-2 useful picks. In that world raising `stream_t` can only lose, so the whole
`stream_t` axis looked flat/bad, and `cap_rows` looked inert because at `stream_t≈11` the tail is
nearly empty by construction (`n_tail≈0`) - the two knobs are NOT independent, and sweeping one
inside the dead zone of the other measures nothing.

### Cost model that fits (per layer, converged placement, 4096 rows)

```
worker assignments ≈ 5.0-7.0 k picks   (~1.3-1.7 per row after promote-on-miss)
streamed experts   ≈ 80-110 of 260    (94% of the assignments)
tail               ≈ 330-990 picks over 290-770 rows (1.14-1.29 picks/tail row)
3090 streamed leg  ≈ 92 us of link per streamed expert (1.17 MB / 12.8 GB/s) + ~1.7 us/pick
B70 tail leg       ≈ 5.9 us per fragment, 4 tokens per fragment, chunks per expert per job
                   => ~12 us per (expert, <=8 picks) chunk, ~9 us per tail row measured
```

So the crossover is an absolute pick count (~20-25 here), which is why `STREAM_T=24` wins at both
2048 and 4096 rows while 16 and 32 are a bit worse: below it, the 3090 pays a whole DMA + launch
per 1-2-pick expert; above it, the B70 pays full fragments for half-empty chunks.

Why the `k0` row is not a big-job bug:

1. `k0` c1024 repeated **bit-identically** (2.325729 twice) → not atomic-order noise.
2. `bench_layer --e 260 --rows {128,512,1024} --bits 2 --hb 4 --i8trace`: stages B..G all OK, and
   `rms_rel` 1.362 / 1.353 / 1.356e-03 at every job size → the plugin computes 1024-row jobs as
   accurately as 128-row ones; nothing is dropped or truncated by a bigger job.
3. The float tier is *further* from this CPU run than the int8 tier (0.30-0.43% vs 0.14-0.32%),
   which is expected: the CPU worker here runs the **AVX2/int8** tier (~1.4% off scalar), so
   "B70 float == scalar reference" does not imply "B70 float == this CPU run". Cross-arm ppl
   deltas at the 0.1-0.3% level track *which tier each pick lands in*, not job slicing.
4. The shipped arm moved 0.06% (2.320567 → 2.321975), inside the recorded ≤0.25% int8 budget.
